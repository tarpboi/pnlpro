Commodity Futures

7 min read · Updated Nov 23, 2026
More in Futures Trading
  • Futures Basics
  • Index Futures (ES, NQ)
  • Commodity Futures
  • Contango, Backwardation & Rollover
  • Futures vs Stocks, CFDs & Perps

Commodity futures are exchange-traded contracts to buy or sell a set quantity of a physical good, such as crude oil, gold, corn or cattle, at a future date. They are the original futures market and remain the main way producers, consumers and speculators set and hedge commodity prices. Unlike index futures, many commodity contracts settle by physical delivery, and prices respond to weather, inventories and seasons as much as to financial news.

Key takeaways

  • Commodity contracts are defined by physical quantity, such as 1,000 barrels of crude or 100 ounces of gold, and the tick value follows from that size.
  • Supply and demand for the physical good drives prices, so government inventory and crop reports are major scheduled events.
  • Many commodities show seasonal patterns, but seasonality is a tendency, not a rule.
  • Physically delivered contracts must be closed or rolled before the first notice day unless you can actually make or take delivery.
  • Commodity futures can be extremely volatile, and the April 2020 crude oil collapse showed that prices can even go negative.

Key contract specifications

The table below lists widely traded US contracts. Tick value is tick size multiplied by contract size.

Contract Symbol Contract size Tick size Tick value
WTI crude oil CL 1,000 barrels $0.01 per barrel $10.00
Micro WTI crude oil MCL 100 barrels $0.01 per barrel $1.00
Henry Hub natural gas NG 10,000 MMBtu $0.001 per MMBtu $10.00
Gold GC 100 troy ounces $0.10 per ounce $10.00
Micro gold MGC 10 troy ounces $0.10 per ounce $1.00
Silver SI 5,000 troy ounces $0.005 per ounce $25.00
Copper HG 25,000 pounds $0.0005 per pound $12.50
Corn ZC 5,000 bushels 1/4 cent per bushel $12.50
Soybeans ZS 5,000 bushels 1/4 cent per bushel $12.50
Chicago wheat ZW 5,000 bushels 1/4 cent per bushel $12.50

Brent crude futures on ICE are also 1,000 barrels. Live cattle futures on CME cover 40,000 pounds. Specs can change, so check the exchange before trading. Contract mechanics such as margin and mark-to-market are explained in futures basics.

Worked example: what a move is worth

  • Crude oil: CL rises from $72.40 to $73.90, a $1.50 move. That is 150 ticks x $10 = $1,500 per contract. On MCL, the same move is $150.
  • Gold: GC falls from $2,410.00 to $2,385.50, a $24.50 move. That is 245 ticks x $10 = $2,450 per contract.
  • Corn: ZC rises from $4.50 to $4.62 per bushel, a 12-cent move. That is 48 quarter-cent ticks x $12.50 = $600 per contract. The notional value at $4.50 is 5,000 x $4.50 = $22,500.
  • Natural gas: NG rises from $3.000 to $3.250, a $0.25 move. That is 250 ticks x $10 = $2,500 per contract.

Natural gas often moves several percent in a day, so a full-size NG contract can gain or lose thousands of dollars in a single session.

Energy

Crude oil is the most traded commodity future. WTI (West Texas Intermediate) is the US benchmark, priced for delivery at Cushing, Oklahoma. Brent is the international benchmark and trades on ICE. Prices respond to OPEC+ production decisions, global growth, the US dollar, refinery demand and geopolitical risk. Natural gas is more regional and highly sensitive to weather and storage levels. See oil and energy trading for the fundamentals.

Metals

Gold trades as both a commodity and a financial asset. It tends to respond to real interest rates, the US dollar, central bank buying and demand for safety. Silver has larger industrial demand and usually moves more in percentage terms. Copper is closely tied to construction, manufacturing and electrification, and is often read as a gauge of global growth. See gold and precious metals.

Agriculture: grains, softs and livestock

Agricultural futures are driven by planting, weather, harvests, export demand and government policy. Grain markets are especially sensitive to growing-season weather in the US, Brazil, Argentina, Ukraine and Russia. Softs such as cocoa and coffee depend on a small number of producing regions, which can lead to dramatic moves when crops fail, as cocoa's surge to record prices in 2024 showed. Livestock prices reflect feed costs, herd sizes and consumer demand.

Seasonality

Many commodities have recurring seasonal patterns linked to the physical world:

  • Natural gas: demand rises in winter for heating, and storage typically builds from spring to autumn.
  • Gasoline: demand tends to peak in the Northern Hemisphere summer driving season.
  • Grains: US corn and soybean prices often carry a "weather premium" during the summer growing season that can fade if crops develop well.

Seasonal tendencies are widely known and often already reflected in the futures curve. They can fail badly in any given year, so they work better as context than as a trading signal on their own.

Inventory and crop reports

Scheduled government reports are among the biggest volatility events in commodity futures.

Report Publisher Typical timing Markets affected
Weekly Petroleum Status Report US Energy Information Administration (EIA) Wednesdays, 10:30 a.m. ET Crude oil, gasoline, heating oil
Weekly Natural Gas Storage Report EIA Thursdays, 10:30 a.m. ET Natural gas
WASDE (World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates) US Department of Agriculture (USDA) Monthly Grains, oilseeds, cotton, livestock
Crop Progress USDA Weekly in the growing season Grains and oilseeds

The American Petroleum Institute (API) also publishes an industry inventory estimate on Tuesday afternoons, the day before the EIA report. Release times shift around holidays, so check the current calendar. As with economic data, the market reacts to the surprise versus expectations, not the raw figure. See news trading and the economic calendar.

Physical delivery risk

Most commodity futures are physically delivered. If you hold a long position into the delivery period, you can be required to accept and pay for the full quantity: 1,000 barrels of crude at Cushing, or 5,000 bushels of corn through an approved warehouse receipt. Retail traders have no way to store or handle these goods.

Key dates to know:

  • First notice day (FND): the first day a short can notify the exchange of intent to deliver. For many contracts, longs holding past this date can be assigned delivery.
  • Last trading day: the final day the contract trades. For WTI crude, trading in a contract month ends a few business days before the 25th calendar day of the preceding month.

Most retail brokers require customers to exit before FND or the last trading day and will liquidate positions that remain, often at a poor price. Some contracts, such as Micro WTI crude oil, are cash-settled, which avoids this problem. Read the contract specification for every product you trade.

The most dramatic example of delivery-related stress came on April 20, 2020, when the May 2020 WTI contract settled at minus $37.63 per barrel the day before it expired. Storage at Cushing was nearly full during the pandemic collapse in demand, and holders who could not take delivery paid to get out. That episode is covered in more detail in contango, backwardation and rollover and leverage risk and black swans.

Common mistakes

  • Holding past first notice day. Forced liquidation or delivery notices are expensive and stressful. Put the date in your calendar.
  • Underestimating volatility. Natural gas, cocoa and silver can move several percent a day. Size positions on the dollar value of a typical move, not on margin.
  • Trading through reports without a plan. EIA and USDA releases can cause instant gaps through stop orders.
  • Treating seasonality as a guarantee. Seasonal patterns are averages that hide many years in which the opposite happened.
  • Ignoring the futures curve. Holding commodity exposure for months means rolling contracts, and the shape of the curve can add or subtract returns.

FAQ

How big is one crude oil futures contract?

One NYMEX WTI crude oil contract (CL) is 1,000 barrels, so each $0.01 tick is worth $10 and each $1 move is worth $1,000. The Micro WTI contract (MCL) is 100 barrels, one-tenth the size.

What happens if you hold a commodity future until expiry?

If the contract is physically delivered, you may be required to make or take delivery of the actual commodity. Most retail brokers close such positions before first notice day to prevent this.

What is the best time to trade crude oil futures?

Liquidity is usually highest during US daytime hours, and the weekly EIA petroleum report on Wednesday at 10:30 a.m. ET often brings the biggest moves of the week. Higher activity means tighter spreads but also faster price swings.

Are commodity futures riskier than stock index futures?

Not always, but many commodities are more volatile than major stock indices and are exposed to supply shocks such as weather, wars and export bans. Physical delivery adds a risk that cash-settled index futures do not have.
