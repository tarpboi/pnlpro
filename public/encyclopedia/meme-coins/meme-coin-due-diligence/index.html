Meme Coin Due Diligence

7 min read · Updated Nov 23, 2026

Meme coin due diligence is a short set of on-chain and social checks that tells you whether a token can be traded safely at all, and how much of its supply sits with people who can dump on you. It cannot tell you whether a coin will go up. What it can do is filter out a large share of rugs, honeypots and insider-controlled tokens in a few minutes, which is often the difference between losing a small bet and losing it to a scam.

Key takeaways

  • Check contract permissions first: mint authority, freeze authority and, on EVM chains, taxes and sell restrictions.
  • Check liquidity: whether LP tokens are burned or locked, and how deep the pool is relative to the market value.
  • Check concentration: top holders excluding the pool, dev wallets, bundled wallets and sniper share.
  • Token checker tools automate much of this, but they miss linked wallets and new tricks, so read their output critically.
  • Passing every check makes a token less likely to be an outright scam; it does not make it a good trade.

The checklist at a glance

Freeze authority (Solana) Revoked Enabled: your tokens can be frozen

Contract permissions

Mint authority

On Solana, a token's mint authority is the address allowed to create new units. If it is still active, the holder can mint billions of new tokens and sell them into the pool, wiping out everyone else. Standard launchpad tokens usually have mint authority revoked at creation. Tokens created outside launchpads may not.

On EVM chains such as Ethereum, Base and BNB Chain, the equivalent is a mint function in the contract that the owner can call. "Renounced ownership" means the owner address has been set to a dead address, but some contracts keep privileged roles elsewhere. See rug pulls and honeypots for the fake-renounce trick.

Freeze authority

Solana tokens can also have a freeze authority, which can freeze any holder's token account so they cannot transfer or sell. A token with active freeze authority can be turned into a honeypot at any time. Revoked is the only acceptable answer for a meme coin.

Token-2022 extensions

Solana's newer Token-2022 standard supports extensions such as transfer fees, transfer hooks and a permanent delegate (an address that can move tokens out of any holder's account). These have legitimate uses, but on a meme coin they are a reason to look much harder or pass.

Taxes and sell restrictions on EVM chains

EVM tokens are custom contracts, so they can include buy and sell taxes, maximum transaction sizes, maximum wallet sizes, blacklists, trading pauses and cooldowns. Taxes the owner can change later are especially dangerous: a token can launch at 0 percent and switch to 99 percent. Simulation tools try a test buy and sell to detect this, but they are not perfect.

Liquidity

After a token graduates from a launchpad or is launched directly on a DEX, its pool holds the quote asset (SOL, ETH, BNB) and the token. Whoever holds the LP tokens can withdraw that liquidity.

  • Burned LP: sent to an address no one controls. The liquidity cannot be withdrawn.
  • Locked LP: held by a time-lock contract until a date. Check how long and on which locker.
  • Unlocked LP: the deployer can pull the pool at any moment, the classic hard rug.

Also compare liquidity to market value. A token with a $2 million market value and $40,000 of liquidity can only absorb small sells before price collapses. See bid-ask spread and liquidity.

Holder concentration

Open the holder list on a block explorer or terminal. Exclude the pool itself, the launchpad's curve account, and burn addresses. Then look at what is left.

Worked example: reading a holder list

A token has a supply of 1 billion. The top holders are:

Holder Share Note
DEX pool 18.0% Exclude
Wallet A 6.5% Funded by the deployer an hour before launch
Wallet B 4.2% Bought in the creation block
Wallet C 4.1% Bought in the creation block
Wallet D 4.0% Bought in the creation block
Wallets E to J 9.6% combined Mixed, unrelated histories

Excluding the pool, the top ten holders own 6.5 + 4.2 + 4.1 + 4.0 + 9.6 = 28.4 percent. On its own that might look acceptable. But wallets A to D are likely one group: A was funded by the deployer, and B, C and D bought in the same block as creation. Together they hold 6.5 + 4.2 + 4.1 + 4.0 = 18.8 percent.

At a $500,000 market value, each token is worth $0.0005, so the cluster's 188 million tokens are worth $94,000 at spot. The pool's 180 million tokens are worth $90,000, and a constant-product pool holds equal value on both sides, so it also holds about $90,000 of SOL. If the cluster sold everything at once, the pool's token side would grow to 368 million, its SOL side would shrink to about $90,000 × 180 / 368 ≈ $44,000, and the cluster would walk away with about $46,000, roughly half the SOL in the pool. The price would fall from $0.0005 to about $44,000 / 368 million ≈ $0.00012, a drop of about 76 percent. That is the real risk the headline "top 10" number hides.

Dev wallets, bundles and snipers

Dev wallet

Terminals usually label the deployer and show whether it still holds tokens. A dev who sold early is common and not automatically a scam, but it removes a party with a reason to support the token. A dev who funded many other holder wallets is a bigger warning.

Bundles

A bundle, in this context, is a group of buys executed together in the same block as the token's creation, often from many fresh wallets. On Solana this is commonly done with Jito bundles, which let several transactions land together in order. The effect is to grab a large share of supply at the lowest price while making the holder list look spread out. Many terminals now flag "bundled" supply as a percentage. A high bundled share means one party can likely dump a large amount.

Snipers

Snipers are bots that bought in the first seconds. If they still hold a large share, expect them to sell into any rally. See sniping new launches.

Following the money

Visual tools such as Bubblemaps show clusters of wallets that sent funds to each other. On-chain explorers let you check where a holder wallet's initial SOL or ETH came from. Many wallets funded from the same source within minutes of each other usually belong to one person. See on-chain analysis.

Socials and volume quality

  • Account age. Same-day accounts are normal for launchpad coins, but a claim to be "the official" token of an existing meme needs an older account behind it.
  • Engagement. Real conversation, not identical replies from new accounts.
  • Volume. Heavy volume from a small number of wallets suggests wash trading; see finding meme coins.

Token checker tools and their limits

Tools such as RugCheck (Solana), Token Sniffer, Honeypot.is, GoPlus-based scanners and the risk panels inside terminals automate many of the checks above. They are valuable for speed. Their limits:

  • They miss linked wallets that were funded through intermediaries or exchanges.
  • They lag new techniques. Scammers test against popular checkers.
  • Scores are heuristics. A "good" score is not a guarantee, and a "warning" may be harmless.
  • Simulations can be fooled. Some EVM honeypots behave normally for small test sells or for the first period after launch.

Use checkers as a first filter, then look at the raw data yourself for anything you plan to size up.

Common mistakes

  • Counting the pool as a holder. Always exclude pool and curve accounts before judging concentration.
  • Trusting "renounced" without checking. Look for other privileged roles or a proxy contract.
  • Skipping checks for trending tokens. Popular tokens get rugged too.
  • Relying on a single tool's score. Cross-check with a block explorer.
  • Treating due diligence as a buy signal. Safe-looking tokens still go to zero, usually because attention moves on.

FAQ

What does revoked mint authority mean?

It means no one can create new units of the token, so the supply is fixed. If mint authority is still active, the holder can mint new tokens and sell them, diluting everyone else.

What is freeze authority on Solana?

Freeze authority lets an address freeze any token account so the holder cannot transfer or sell. For meme coins, it should be revoked; an active freeze authority can turn a token into a honeypot.

What is a safe top 10 holder percentage for a meme coin?

There is no safe number. After excluding the pool and burn addresses, lower is better, but what matters more is whether several top wallets are linked, since one group spread across many wallets can dump just as hard as one large wallet.

Are token checkers like RugCheck reliable?

They are useful for catching obvious problems quickly, such as active mint or freeze authority and unlocked liquidity. They can miss linked wallets and new scam techniques, so treat a good score as a first filter, not a guarantee.
