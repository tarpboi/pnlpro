Meme Coin Lifecycle

The meme coin lifecycle is the typical sequence a token moves through, from creation on a launchpad to graduation into a decentralized exchange pool, an early run, a period of consolidation, sometimes a centralized exchange listing, and finally a long decay. Most tokens die in the first stage. Knowing which stage a token is in, and who is likely buying and selling there, is one of the most useful pieces of context a meme coin trader can have.

Key takeaways

  • The large majority of tokens never leave the first stage; they stall on the bonding curve and go quiet within hours.
  • Graduation to a DEX pool is a liquidity event, not a quality signal, and is often followed by heavy selling from early buyers.
  • The early run after graduation is where most of the money changes hands, and also where most retail buyers arrive late.
  • Centralized exchange listings tend to come after much of the rise, and can mark a top as often as a new leg up.
  • Almost every meme coin ends in a slow decay as attention moves on, even the ones that had a big run.

The stages at a glance

Stage Typical duration Main buyers Main sellers Key risk
1. Launch Seconds to minutes Creator, bundled wallets, snipers Snipers flipping Insider supply, instant dump
2. Bonding curve Minutes to hours Early degens, bots, copy traders Snipers, dev Never graduating
3. Graduation One transaction Pool is seeded automatically Curve buyers taking profit Post-migration dump
4. Early run Hours to days Screener traffic, KOL followers Insiders, early holders Buying the top
5. Consolidation Days to weeks Dip buyers, community Tired holders Slow bleed
6. CEX listing (rare) One event New audience on exchanges Early on-chain holders Listing top
7. Decay Weeks to forever Very few Everyone left Illiquidity

Durations vary enormously. A token can go through stages 1 to 4 in under an hour on a busy day.

Stage 1: Launch

A creator picks a name, ticker, image and short description, then deploys the token on a launchpad. On pump.fun-style platforms this costs very little and takes under a minute. The creator can make the first buy in the same transaction.

In the first seconds, several groups compete:

  • The creator, who may buy openly or through many wallets in the same block (a "bundle") to hide how much supply they control.
  • Snipers, who run bots that buy in the first block or first few seconds. See sniping new launches.
  • Insider groups who coordinated the launch in advance.

Holder behavior here is almost purely short-term. Snipers want a quick 2x to 5x and sell into the first wave of manual buyers. Many launches end at this stage: the creator or snipers sell, the price collapses back down the curve, and no one else arrives.

Stage 2: The bonding curve phase

While a token is on the bonding curve, the launchpad's contract is the only market. Price rises as SOL (or ETH, BNB and so on) flows in and falls as tokens are sold back. There is no external liquidity pool yet.

Traders watch a "progress" percentage toward graduation. Analytics terminals sort tokens into columns such as "new", "about to graduate" and "graduated", which concentrates attention on tokens near the threshold. That attention can itself push a token over the line.

Typical behavior:

  • Fresh tokens see bursts of small buys from bots and manual traders scanning new listings.
  • A token that gains momentum attracts copy traders who follow known wallets.
  • Developers often sell some or all of their allocation during this phase. A "dev sold" label on a terminal is common and not always fatal, but it removes one reason to expect support.

By most published estimates, only around 1 to 2 percent or fewer of launchpad tokens ever complete the curve.

Stage 3: Graduation (migration)

When the curve reaches its threshold, the launchpad automatically moves the token to a regular AMM pool. Part of the token supply and the collected SOL are deposited as liquidity, and the liquidity provider (LP) tokens are typically burned or locked so the creator cannot withdraw them. On pump.fun, graduated tokens have gone to Raydium in the past and to the platform's own PumpSwap pool since March 2025; other launchpads use other DEXs. Check the current destination on the platform you use.

Graduation matters for three reasons:

  1. New tools see the token. It now appears on DEX screeners and aggregators, which brings a new audience.
  2. The market structure changes. Price now follows the pool's constant-product formula and can be arbitraged across venues. See DeFi trading.
  3. Curve buyers often sell. Many people who bought on the curve planned to sell at graduation. A sharp drop right after migration is common.

Stage 4: The early run

If a token survives the post-migration selling and keeps attracting attention, it can enter a run. This is the phase that produces the screenshots: market value going from tens of thousands of dollars to millions within hours.

What drives it:

  • Trending lists on DEX screeners, some of which can be boosted with paid placement.
  • Posts by influencers, sometimes paid or holding tokens they received early. See KOLs, insiders and cabals.
  • A narrative that fits the moment, such as a news event or a meme already spreading elsewhere.

Who sells: insiders and early holders take profit into the strength. The chart often shows several sharp pullbacks of 30 to 60 percent along the way, each one shaking out late buyers. Most retail traders who find the token through public channels arrive somewhere in the second half of the run.

Worked example: where the money goes

A token graduates at a market value of about $70,000. An insider group holds 15 percent of supply, bought for an average of $4,000 in total on the curve. The token runs to a $3 million market value.

  • The insiders' 15 percent is worth $450,000 on paper at the top.
  • They cannot sell it all at that price, because selling moves the price down. Suppose they sell gradually during the run at an average market value of $1.8 million. Their 15 percent then brings in about $270,000, before slippage and fees.
  • Every dollar of that comes from buyers who paid between $70,000 and $3 million in market value terms.

If the token then decays to a $300,000 market value, a buyer who entered at $2 million in market value is down 85 percent. That is the basic arithmetic of the run: early supply is sold to later attention.

Stage 5: Consolidation

After the first peak, surviving tokens often trade sideways in a wide range for days or weeks. Volume falls. The community tries to sustain interest with memes, small campaigns and new listings on aggregators. Some tokens build a second leg from here, usually on a fresh catalyst. Most drift lower as holders get bored and rotate into newer tokens.

Stage 6: Centralized exchange listing

A small fraction of meme coins get listed on centralized exchanges (CEXs). Listings bring access to buyers who never use on-chain wallets and, on some exchanges, to perpetual futures that allow shorting. See CEX vs DEX.

The common pattern is a spike on the announcement followed by selling as on-chain holders use the new liquidity to exit. A listing typically arrives after most of the rise, not before it. The availability of shorting through perpetuals can also add selling pressure.

Stage 7: Decay

Nearly every meme coin ends here. Volume dries up, liquidity thins, and each sell moves the price further. A handful of coins become long-lived cultural tokens, but even those usually trade far below their peaks for long periods.

A useful mental model: the expected path for a token you are holding is down, unless new attention keeps arriving. Plan exits accordingly; see risk and exits.

Common mistakes

  • Treating graduation as validation. It only means enough money flowed into the curve; insiders can push a token over the threshold themselves.
  • Buying the CEX listing announcement. The listing often marks heavy selling by on-chain holders.
  • Assuming a pullback is a dip to buy. In the decay stage, every bounce is an exit for someone.
  • Ignoring the stage when sizing. Earlier stages carry more rug risk; later stages carry more top risk. Neither is safe.
  • Holding through decay out of loyalty. Community spirit does not create buyers.

FAQ

What does it mean when a meme coin graduates?

Graduation means the token has filled its launchpad's bonding curve, so the platform moves the collected funds and part of the supply into a regular DEX liquidity pool. After that, it trades like any other AMM token and shows up on DEX screeners.

Why does price often drop after a meme coin migrates?

Many traders who bought on the bonding curve planned to sell at graduation, and new liquidity in the pool gives them an easy exit. Unless fresh buyers arrive quickly, that selling pushes the price down.

How long does a meme coin pump last?

There is no fixed length. Many runs last hours, some a few days, and only a tiny number sustain attention for weeks or months; the long-term path for most tokens is a slow decay.

Is a CEX listing good for a meme coin?

It brings new buyers and easier trading, but it often comes after most of the rise and gives early holders liquidity to sell. Price frequently falls after the initial listing spike.
