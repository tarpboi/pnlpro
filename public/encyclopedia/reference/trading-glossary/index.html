Trading Glossary A–Z

493 terms · Updated Nov 23, 2026
More in Reference
  • Trading Glossary A–Z

This glossary defines more than 300 terms used across stocks, forex, futures, options, crypto, meme coins, prediction markets, algorithmic trading, risk management, psychology and the business of trading. Each entry gives a short, plain definition and links to the full encyclopedia article where one covers the topic in depth.

ABCDEFGHIJ–KLMNOPQRSTUVWX–Z

A

  • A-book and B-book: Two ways a retail broker can handle client orders. An A-book broker passes trades to outside liquidity providers, while a B-book broker takes the other side itself and profits when clients lose. See Forex Brokers.
  • Absorption: In order flow analysis, a situation where large resting orders soak up aggressive buying or selling without the price moving much. It can signal that a level is being defended. See Order Flow Trading.
  • Accumulation/distribution: A volume-based indicator that estimates whether an asset is being bought (accumulated) or sold (distributed) by weighting volume according to where the price closes within each bar's range. See Volume Analysis.
  • Address poisoning: A crypto scam in which an attacker sends tiny transactions from a wallet address that looks like one you use, hoping you will copy the wrong address from your history. See Wallet Security.
  • Adverse selection: The risk of trading mainly with people who know more than you, so your fills tend to be the ones that lose. It is the main hazard for market makers. See Market Making.
  • After-hours trading: Trading in a stock after the regular session closes, usually with thinner liquidity and wider spreads. See Pre-Market & After-Hours.
  • Airdrop: A distribution of free tokens to wallet addresses, often to reward early users of a crypto project. See Airdrops & Points.
  • Algorithmic trading: Using computer programs to decide on and place trades according to predefined rules. See Algorithmic Trading Intro.
  • Altcoin: Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin. Altcoins tend to be more volatile and less liquid than Bitcoin. See Altcoin Trading.
  • American odds: A betting odds format where positive numbers show the profit on a 100 stake and negative numbers show the stake needed to win 100. See Pricing & Probability.
  • American option: An option that can be exercised at any time up to expiry. Most US single-stock options are American style. See Options Basics.
  • Anchored VWAP: A volume-weighted average price calculated from a chosen starting point, such as an earnings date or a major high, rather than from the session open. See VWAP.
  • Anchoring: A cognitive bias in which a trader relies too heavily on a reference number, such as their entry price or a past high. See Cognitive Biases.
  • Arbitrage: Profiting from a price difference for the same or equivalent asset in different places or forms. True riskless arbitrage is rare and short-lived. See Arbitrage Strategies.
  • Ask: The lowest price at which someone is currently willing to sell. Also called the offer. See Spread & Liquidity.
  • Asset class: A group of investments with similar characteristics, such as stocks, bonds, currencies, commodities or crypto. See Financial Markets Overview.
  • Assignment: The obligation of an option seller to fulfill the contract when the buyer exercises, by selling (for a call) or buying (for a put) the underlying. See Expiration & Assignment.
  • At the money (ATM): An option whose strike price is at or very close to the current price of the underlying. See Options Basics.
  • At-the-market offering (ATM offering): A way for a company to sell new shares gradually into the market at current prices, which can dilute existing holders. See Penny Stocks & Small Caps.
  • Auto-deleveraging (ADL): A last-resort mechanism on crypto derivatives exchanges that closes profitable traders' positions when the insurance fund cannot cover a liquidated trader's losses. See Perpetual Futures.
  • Automated market maker (AMM): A smart contract that quotes prices from a pool of tokens using a formula, letting users swap without an order book. See DeFi Trading.
  • Average True Range (ATR): An indicator that measures the average size of price bars, including gaps, over a set period. It is widely used to set stops and size positions. See ATR & Volatility.
  • Average Directional Index (ADX): An indicator that measures the strength of a trend regardless of its direction. See Trends & Market Structure.
  • Avellaneda–Stoikov model: An academic market-making framework that shifts a market maker's quotes based on inventory and volatility around a "reservation price." See Market Making.

B

  • Back and lay: On a betting exchange, backing means betting that an outcome will happen, and laying means betting that it will not, acting as the bookmaker. See Betting Exchange Trading.
  • Backtesting: Testing a trading strategy on historical data to see how it would have performed. Results are often better than live trading because of hidden biases. See Backtesting.
  • Backwardation: A futures market in which later-dated contracts trade below nearer ones or below spot. See Contango, Backwardation & Rollover.
  • Balance sheet: A financial statement listing what a company owns (assets), what it owes (liabilities) and shareholders' equity at a point in time. See Earnings & Financials.
  • Base currency: The first currency in a forex pair, the one being bought or sold. In EUR/USD, the euro is the base. See Currency Pairs.
  • Basis: The difference between a futures price and the spot price of the same asset. See Contango, Backwardation & Rollover.
  • Basis point: One hundredth of a percentage point, or 0.01 percent. Interest rates and spreads are often quoted in basis points.
  • Bear market: A prolonged fall in prices, often defined informally as a decline of 20 percent or more from a recent high.
  • Bear trap: A move that appears to break down below support, drawing in sellers, before reversing sharply higher. See Support & Resistance.
  • Beta: A measure of how much an asset tends to move relative to a benchmark. A beta of 1.5 means it has historically moved about 1.5 times as much as the market. See Correlation & Portfolio Risk.
  • Bid: The highest price at which someone is currently willing to buy. See Spread & Liquidity.
  • Bid-ask spread: The gap between the best bid and the best ask. It is a cost paid on every round trip. See Spread & Liquidity.
  • Binary option: A contract that pays a fixed amount if a condition is met and nothing otherwise. Prediction market event contracts work this way, but offshore "binary options" sold to retail are often predatory. See Prediction Markets Explained.
  • Black swan: A rare, extreme and largely unexpected event with a major market impact. See Leverage Risk & Black Swans.
  • Black-Scholes model: A widely used formula for pricing European options from the underlying price, strike, time, interest rates and volatility. See Implied Volatility.
  • Blue chip: A large, well-established company with a long record of stable earnings. See Stock Market Basics.
  • Bollinger Bands: An indicator that plots bands, typically two standard deviations, above and below a 20-period moving average. See Bollinger Bands.
  • Bond: A loan to a government or company that pays interest and returns the principal at maturity. See Bonds & Rates.
  • Bonding curve: A formula that sets a token's price based on how many tokens have been bought, used by many meme coin launchpads. See Launchpads & Bonding Curves.
  • Bracket order: An entry order packaged with both a take-profit and a stop-loss, so one exit cancels the other. See Order Types.
  • Break of structure: A price move beyond a prior swing high or low that suggests the trend may be continuing or changing. See Trends & Market Structure.
  • Breakeven: The price at which a trade neither makes nor loses money after costs.
  • Breakout: A move through a support or resistance level, often expected to continue in the same direction. See Support & Resistance.
  • Brent: A major crude oil benchmark based on North Sea oil, used to price much of the world's crude. See Oil & Energy.
  • Broker: A firm that gives clients access to markets and executes their orders. See Brokers & Exchanges.
  • BTC dominance: Bitcoin's share of the total crypto market capitalization. See Crypto Market Cycles.
  • Bull market: A prolonged rise in prices.
  • Bull trap: A move that appears to break out above resistance, drawing in buyers, before reversing lower. See Support & Resistance.
  • Bundling: In meme coins, buying a large share of a new token's supply across many wallets in the same block, often by insiders, to hide concentration. See Meme Coin Due Diligence.
  • Burn: Permanently removing tokens from circulation by sending them to an address no one controls. See Tokenomics & Unlocks.
  • Butterfly spread: An options strategy using three strikes that profits most if the underlying finishes near the middle strike. See Iron Condors & Butterflies.
  • Buyback: A company repurchasing its own shares, reducing the share count. See Stock Market Basics.

C

  • Cabal: An informal group of insiders or promoters who coordinate buying and selling of a token, often at followers' expense. See KOLs, Insiders & Cabals.
  • Call option: A contract giving the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy the underlying at the strike price before or at expiry. See Options Basics.
  • Candlestick: A chart bar showing the open, high, low and close for a period, with a body between open and close and wicks to the extremes. See Reading Candlesticks.
  • Capital gains: Profit from selling an asset for more than you paid. Tax treatment varies by country and holding period. See Trading Taxes Overview.
  • Capitulation: A burst of heavy selling as many holders give up at once, sometimes marking a low.
  • Carry trade: Borrowing in a low-interest currency to hold a higher-interest one, aiming to earn the rate difference. See Carry Trade.
  • Cash-and-carry trade: Buying an asset in the spot market and selling a futures or perpetual contract against it to earn the basis or funding. See Funding Rates.
  • Cash-secured put: Selling a put option while holding enough cash to buy the shares if assigned. See Covered Calls & CSPs.
  • Cash settlement: Settling a derivative by paying the cash difference rather than delivering the underlying asset. See Futures Basics.
  • Centralized exchange (CEX): A crypto exchange run by a company that holds customers' funds and matches orders on its own systems. See CEX vs DEX.
  • CFD (contract for difference): A contract with a broker that pays the change in an asset's price without owning it. CFDs are banned for retail clients in the US. See CFDs & Spread Betting.
  • CFTC: The Commodity Futures Trading Commission, the US regulator of futures, options on futures, swaps and regulated event contracts. See Regulators & Investor Protection.
  • Chainlink: A decentralized oracle network that publishes price data and other information to blockchains. Some prediction market crypto rounds settle on its price streams. See Resolution & Oracles.
  • Challenge (prop firm): A paid evaluation in which a trader must hit a profit target without breaking loss limits to get a funded account. See Prop Firms & Funded Accounts.
  • Chart pattern: A recognizable shape in price charts, such as a triangle or head and shoulders, that traders use to anticipate moves. See Chart Patterns.
  • Circuit breaker: An automatic pause in trading after a large price move, used by exchanges and also built into trading bots and personal rules.
  • Circulating supply: The number of tokens currently available to trade, as opposed to the total or maximum supply. See Tokenomics & Unlocks.
  • Clearing house: An entity that stands between buyers and sellers of exchange-traded contracts, guaranteeing settlement and collecting margin. See Futures Basics.
  • Co-location: Placing trading servers in or next to an exchange's data center to reduce latency. See High-Frequency Trading.
  • Cointegration: A statistical relationship in which two price series drift apart but tend to return to a stable spread. It is the basis of many pairs trades. See Pairs Trading & Stat Arb.
  • Commission: A fee charged by a broker for executing a trade. See Trading Costs & Fees.
  • Commodity: A raw material or primary product, such as oil, gold or wheat, traded in bulk. See Commodities Trading.
  • Commitments of Traders (COT) report: A weekly US report showing how different groups of traders are positioned in futures markets. See Sentiment Indicators.
  • Complementary outcomes: In a binary prediction market, YES and NO are complements whose payouts always add up to $1. See Pricing & Probability.
  • Conditional tokens: A token design used by some crypto prediction markets in which one unit of collateral can be split into one YES and one NO share, and the pair can be merged back into collateral. See Prediction Market Arbitrage.
  • Confirmation bias: The tendency to seek out information that supports what you already believe and ignore what contradicts it. See Cognitive Biases.
  • Consistency rule: A prop firm rule limiting how much of total profit can come from a single day or trade. See Prop Firms & Funded Accounts.
  • Consolidation: A period when price moves sideways in a range after a trend.
  • Contango: A futures market in which later-dated contracts trade above nearer ones or above spot, often reflecting storage and financing costs. See Contango, Backwardation & Rollover.
  • Contract multiplier: The number of units of the underlying controlled by one contract. A standard US equity option usually covers 100 shares. See Options Basics.
  • Copy trading: Automatically mirroring the trades of another trader or wallet. See Copy & Social Trading.
  • Correction: A decline of roughly 10 percent or more from a recent high, short of a bear market.
  • Correlation: A measure, from −1 to +1, of how closely two assets move together. See Correlation & Portfolio Risk.
  • Covered call: Selling a call option against shares you own to collect premium, capping your upside. See Covered Calls & CSPs.
  • CPI (Consumer Price Index): A measure of the change in prices paid by consumers for a basket of goods and services, a key inflation gauge. See Economic Indicators.
  • Crack spread: The price difference between crude oil and the refined products made from it, a proxy for refining margins. See Oil & Energy.
  • Creation and redemption: The process by which large participants create or redeem ETF shares in exchange for the underlying assets, keeping the ETF price close to its net asset value. See ETFs & Index Trading.
  • Credit spread: An options spread that collects net premium up front, such as a bull put or bear call spread. See Vertical Spreads.
  • Cross (currency pair): A currency pair that does not include the US dollar, such as EUR/GBP. See Currency Pairs.
  • Cross margin: A margin mode where all positions share one pool of collateral, so a loss in one can draw on the whole account. See Leverage & Margin.
  • Cross-venue arbitrage: Buying an outcome on one platform and its opposite on another when the combined price is below the payout. See Prediction Market Arbitrage.
  • Cumulative delta: A running total of the difference between volume traded at the ask and at the bid, used to gauge aggressive buying or selling. See Order Flow Trading.
  • Cup and handle: A chart pattern shaped like a rounded bottom followed by a small pullback, often read as bullish. See Chart Patterns.
  • Curve fitting: Tuning a strategy so closely to past data that it captures noise rather than a real edge. See Overfitting.
  • Custody: Who holds and safeguards your assets, whether a broker, an exchange or you in your own wallet. See Brokers & Exchanges.

D

  • Daily loss limit: A cap on how much a trader allows themselves, or a prop firm allows, to lose in one day before stopping. See Risk Management Basics.
  • Dark pool: A private trading venue where orders are not displayed publicly before execution, often used for large institutional trades.
  • Data snooping: Testing many ideas on the same data until one looks good by chance. See Overfitting.
  • Day trading: Opening and closing positions within the same trading day. Most retail day traders lose money over time. See Day Trading Stocks.
  • Days to cover: Short interest divided by average daily volume, an estimate of how long shorts would take to buy back. See Short Selling.
  • Dead cat bounce: A brief recovery in a falling market that is followed by further declines.
  • Death cross: When a shorter moving average, commonly the 50-day, crosses below a longer one, commonly the 200-day. See Moving Averages.
  • Decentralized exchange (DEX): A crypto exchange that runs on smart contracts, letting users trade from their own wallets. See CEX vs DEX.
  • Decimal odds: An odds format showing the total return per unit staked, including the stake. Decimal odds of 4.00 imply a 25 percent probability. See Pricing & Probability.
  • DeFi (decentralized finance): Financial services such as trading, lending and borrowing that run on public blockchains without a central intermediary. See DeFi Trading.
  • Delta: How much an option's price is expected to change for a one-unit move in the underlying. See The Greeks.
  • Delta hedging: Offsetting an options position's delta by trading the underlying, as market makers commonly do. See Gamma Exposure & Dealers.
  • Depeg: When a stablecoin's market price moves away from the value it is meant to track. See Stablecoins.
  • Derivative: A financial contract whose value is derived from an underlying asset, rate or event, such as a future, option or swap. See Financial Markets Overview.
  • Designated contract market (DCM): A US exchange registered with the CFTC to list futures, options and event contracts. See Regulation.
  • Dev wallet: The wallet of a token's creator. Large dev holdings are a risk because they can be sold on buyers. See Meme Coin Due Diligence.
  • Dilution: A fall in existing shareholders' ownership percentage when a company issues new shares. See Penny Stocks & Small Caps.
  • Dispute (oracle): A challenge to a proposed prediction market outcome, usually backed by a bond, which can escalate to a vote. See Resolution & Oracles.
  • Disposition effect: The tendency to sell winners too early and hold losers too long. See Cognitive Biases.
  • Divergence: When price and an indicator such as RSI move in opposite directions, sometimes read as a sign of a weakening trend. See RSI.
  • Dividend: A payment a company makes to shareholders out of its profits. See Stock Market Basics.
  • Dividend yield: Annual dividends per share divided by the share price. See Valuation Ratios.
  • Doji: A candlestick whose open and close are almost the same, showing indecision. See Candlestick Patterns.
  • DOM (depth of market): A live ladder showing resting buy and sell orders at each price level. See Order Flow Trading.
  • Dot plot: A chart published by the US Federal Reserve showing each policymaker's projection for future interest rates. See Central Banks & Rates.
  • Double top and double bottom: Chart patterns in which price tests the same high or low twice and fails, often read as reversal signals. See Chart Patterns.
  • Dovish: Describes a central bank stance that leans toward lower interest rates or looser policy. See Central Banks & Rates.
  • Drainer: Malicious code, usually behind a phishing site, that empties a crypto wallet once the user signs a harmful approval. See Wallet Security.
  • Drawdown: The decline in account value from a peak to a subsequent low. See Drawdown & Risk of Ruin.
  • Duration: A measure of a bond's price sensitivity to changes in interest rates. See Bonds & Rates.

E

  • E-mini: A family of electronically traded index futures, such as the E-mini S&P 500, worth $50 times the index. See Index Futures (ES, NQ).
  • Earnings per share (EPS): A company's net profit divided by its number of shares. See Earnings & Financials.
  • Earnings season: The weeks after each quarter end when most public companies report results. See Earnings Trading.
  • ECN (electronic communication network): A system that matches orders between participants. Brokers marketed as ECN usually pass orders to outside liquidity for a commission. See Forex Brokers.
  • Economic calendar: A schedule of upcoming economic data releases and central bank events. See News Trading.
  • Elliott Wave: A theory that markets move in repeating patterns of five waves with the trend and three against it. See Elliott Wave & Wyckoff.
  • EMA (exponential moving average): A moving average that gives more weight to recent prices. See Moving Averages.
  • Engulfing pattern: A two-candle pattern in which the second candle's body fully covers the first's, read as a possible reversal. See Candlestick Patterns.
  • Equity (account): The current value of a trading account, including open profits and losses.
  • ESMA: The European Securities and Markets Authority, which coordinates securities regulation in the EU and set retail CFD leverage limits. See CFDs & Spread Betting.
  • ETF (exchange-traded fund): A fund that holds a basket of assets and trades on an exchange like a stock. See ETFs & Index Trading.
  • ETN (exchange-traded note): A debt security that tracks an index and trades like a stock, carrying the issuer's credit risk. See ETFs & Index Trading.
  • European option: An option that can be exercised only at expiry. Many index options are European style. See Options Basics.
  • EV/EBITDA: Enterprise value divided by earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a valuation ratio that accounts for debt. See Valuation Ratios.
  • Event contract: A contract whose payoff depends on whether a specified event happens, typically paying $1 for YES and $0 for NO. See Prediction Markets Explained.
  • Exhaustion gap: A price gap late in a trend that is followed by a reversal. See Gap Trading.
  • Exit liquidity: Buyers whose purchases let earlier holders sell. Retail traders often become exit liquidity for insiders. See KOLs, Insiders & Cabals.
  • Exotic pair: A currency pair that combines a major currency with that of a smaller or emerging economy, usually with wide spreads. See Currency Pairs.
  • Expectancy: The average amount a strategy is expected to win or lose per trade, combining win rate and payoff. See Risk/Reward & Expectancy.
  • Expected move: The price range implied by options prices for the underlying over a period, often around earnings. See Implied Volatility.
  • Expiration: The date and time an option or futures contract ceases to exist. See Expiration & Assignment.
  • Extrinsic value: The part of an option's premium above its intrinsic value, reflecting time and volatility. See Options Basics.

F

  • Fair value gap: In smart money concepts, a three-candle imbalance where price moved so fast that the first and third candles' wicks do not overlap. See Smart Money Concepts (ICT).
  • Fakeout: A breakout that quickly reverses back into the prior range. See Support & Resistance.
  • Fat finger: An order entry error, such as typing too many zeros, that causes an unintended trade.
  • FCA: The Financial Conduct Authority, the UK's main financial regulator. See Regulators & Investor Protection.
  • FCF yield: Free cash flow per share divided by share price. See Valuation Ratios.
  • Fear and greed index: A composite gauge of market sentiment built from several indicators. Versions exist for stocks and crypto. See Sentiment Indicators.
  • Federal Reserve (Fed): The central bank of the United States, which sets US monetary policy. See Central Banks & Rates.
  • Fibonacci retracement: Horizontal levels at ratios such as 38.2, 50 and 61.8 percent of a prior move, used as potential support or resistance. Evidence that they work is weak. See Fibonacci Tools.
  • Fill: The execution of an order, fully or partly.
  • FINRA: The Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, a US self-regulatory organization overseeing broker-dealers. See Regulators & Investor Protection.
  • Flag and pennant: Short continuation patterns that form as price pauses after a sharp move. See Chart Patterns.
  • Float: The number of a company's shares available for public trading. Low-float stocks can move violently. See Penny Stocks & Small Caps.
  • FOMO (fear of missing out): The urge to jump into a trade because prices are moving and others seem to be profiting. See FOMO & Revenge Trading.
  • Footprint chart: A chart that shows volume traded at the bid and ask at each price inside every bar. See Order Flow Trading.
  • Forex (FX): The foreign exchange market, where currencies are traded. See Forex Basics.
  • Forward guidance: A central bank's public signals about the likely path of future policy. See Central Banks & Rates.
  • Forward P/E: Share price divided by expected earnings per share over the next year. See Valuation Ratios.
  • Fractional Kelly: Betting a fraction, such as half, of the size the Kelly criterion suggests, to reduce volatility and the cost of estimation error. See Kelly Criterion.
  • Freeze authority: A permission on some tokens, notably on Solana, that lets the creator freeze holders' tokens so they cannot be sold. See Meme Coin Due Diligence.
  • Front-running: Trading ahead of a known pending order to profit from its price impact. On blockchains, it is a form of MEV. See MEV & Sandwich Attacks.
  • Fully diluted valuation (FDV): A token's price multiplied by its maximum supply, including tokens not yet released. See Tokenomics & Unlocks.
  • Fundamental analysis: Assessing an asset's value from economic, financial and other underlying factors. See Fundamental Analysis Intro.
  • Funded account: A trading account backed by a prop firm's capital, usually after passing an evaluation. See Prop Firms & Funded Accounts.
  • Funding rate: A periodic payment between long and short holders of perpetual futures that keeps the contract price near the spot price. See Funding Rates.
  • Futures contract: A standardized agreement to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a future date, traded on an exchange. See Futures Basics.

G

  • Gambler's fallacy: The mistaken belief that past random outcomes change the odds of the next one, such as expecting a win after a losing streak. See Cognitive Biases.
  • Gamma: How much an option's delta changes for a one-unit move in the underlying. See The Greeks.
  • Gamma exposure (GEX): An estimate of how much options dealers may need to buy or sell the underlying as it moves, based on their assumed positions. See Gamma Exposure & Dealers.
  • Gamma squeeze: A rapid price rise driven partly by options dealers buying the underlying to hedge calls they have sold. See Gamma Exposure & Dealers.
  • Gap: A jump in price between one bar's close and the next bar's open, with no trading in between. See Gap Trading.
  • Gas fee: The fee paid to process a transaction on a blockchain. See Trading Costs & Fees.
  • GDP (gross domestic product): The total value of goods and services produced in an economy over a period. See Economic Indicators.
  • Gold/silver ratio: The number of ounces of silver needed to buy one ounce of gold. See Gold & Precious Metals.
  • Golden cross: When a shorter moving average, commonly the 50-day, crosses above a longer one, commonly the 200-day. See Moving Averages.
  • Graduation (migration): The point at which a meme coin leaves its launchpad bonding curve and moves to a regular DEX liquidity pool. See Meme Coin Lifecycle.
  • Greeks: Measures of an option's sensitivity to price, time, volatility and interest rates: delta, gamma, theta, vega and rho. See The Greeks.
  • Greening up: On a betting exchange, placing an offsetting bet so the trader makes the same profit whatever the outcome. See Betting Exchange Trading.
  • GTC (good till canceled): A time-in-force instruction that keeps an order open until it fills or is canceled. See Order Types.
  • Guaranteed stop: A stop-loss order, offered by some brokers for a fee, that is guaranteed to fill at the set price even if the market gaps. See Stop-Loss Strategies.

H

  • Halt: A temporary suspension of trading in a security, often after big news or a sharp price move. See Penny Stocks & Small Caps.
  • Halving: The scheduled cut in new Bitcoin issued per block, which happens roughly every four years. See Crypto Market Cycles.
  • Hammer: A candlestick with a small body near the top and a long lower wick, read as possible buying pressure after a decline. See Candlestick Patterns.
  • Harami: A two-candle pattern in which a small candle forms inside the body of the previous larger one. See Candlestick Patterns.
  • Hard-to-borrow: A stock that is scarce to borrow for short selling, typically carrying high borrow fees. See Short Selling.
  • Hardware wallet: A physical device that stores crypto private keys offline. See Wallet Security.
  • Hawkish: Describes a central bank stance that leans toward higher interest rates or tighter policy. See Central Banks & Rates.
  • Head and shoulders: A reversal chart pattern with three peaks, the middle one highest. See Chart Patterns.
  • Hedge: A position taken to reduce the risk of another position. See Long & Short.
  • Hedge ratio: The size of one leg relative to the other in a hedge or pairs trade. See Pairs Trading & Stat Arb.
  • Heikin-Ashi: A chart type that averages price data to smooth candles and make trends easier to see. See Technical Analysis Intro.
  • High-frequency trading (HFT): Automated trading at very high speed, holding positions for fractions of a second to minutes. See High-Frequency Trading.
  • High-volume node (HVN) and low-volume node (LVN): Price levels in a volume profile where much or little trading occurred. See Volume & Market Profile.
  • Hindsight bias: The tendency to believe, after the fact, that an outcome was predictable. See Cognitive Biases.
  • Historical volatility: The actual volatility of an asset's past returns, usually annualized. See ATR & Volatility.
  • Honeypot: A malicious token contract that lets people buy but blocks or heavily taxes selling. See Rug Pulls & Honeypots.
  • Hot wallet: A crypto wallet connected to the internet, convenient for trading but more exposed to hacks. See Wallet Security.

I

  • Iceberg order: A large order that shows only a small part of its size in the order book at a time. See Order Types.
  • ICT (Inner Circle Trader): The name of an online educator whose terms, such as order blocks and fair value gaps, form the basis of "smart money concepts." See Smart Money Concepts (ICT).
  • Impermanent loss: The shortfall a liquidity provider in an AMM suffers compared with simply holding the tokens, when their relative prices change. See DeFi Trading.
  • Implementation shortfall: The difference between the price when a trading decision was made and the final execution price, including costs. See Execution Algorithms.
  • Implied probability: The probability of an outcome suggested by its price or odds. A $1 contract at 30 cents implies about 30 percent. See Pricing & Probability.
  • Implied volatility (IV): The volatility level that makes an options pricing model match the option's market price. See Implied Volatility.
  • In the money (ITM): A call with a strike below the underlying price, or a put with a strike above it. See Options Basics.
  • Income statement: A financial statement showing a company's revenue, expenses and profit over a period. See Earnings & Financials.
  • Index: A measure of a group of assets' combined performance, such as the S&P 500. See Stock Index Trading.
  • Index futures: Futures contracts on a stock index. See Index Futures (ES, NQ).
  • Initial margin: The collateral required to open a leveraged position. See Leverage & Margin.
  • Insider trading: Trading on material non-public information in breach of a duty or law. It is illegal in most regulated markets.
  • Insurance fund: A pool held by a derivatives exchange to cover losses when liquidated positions cannot be closed at better than bankruptcy price. See Perpetual Futures.
  • Interest rate differential: The difference between two countries' interest rates, a key driver of currency moves. See What Moves Currencies.
  • Intermarket analysis: Studying relationships between asset classes, such as bonds, stocks, currencies and commodities. See Intermarket Analysis.
  • Intrinsic value (option): The amount by which an option is in the money. Out-of-the-money options have zero intrinsic value. See Options Basics.
  • Inventory risk: The risk that a market maker builds up a one-sided position that loses value. See Market Making (Prediction Markets).
  • Inverse contract: A crypto derivative margined and settled in the coin itself rather than in a stablecoin. See Perpetual Futures.
  • Inverse ETF: An ETF designed to move opposite to its index on a daily basis. See ETFs & Index Trading.
  • IOC (immediate or cancel): An order instruction to fill whatever is possible immediately and cancel the rest. See Order Types.
  • Iowa Electronic Markets: A small-stakes, real-money research prediction market run by the University of Iowa since 1988. See Prediction Markets Explained.
  • IPO (initial public offering): A company's first sale of shares to the public. See Stock Market Basics.
  • Iron condor: An options strategy combining a bull put spread and a bear call spread, profiting if the underlying stays in a range. See Iron Condors & Butterflies.
  • Isolated margin: A margin mode where each position has its own collateral, limiting losses to that amount. See Liquidations.
  • IV crush: A sharp fall in implied volatility after an expected event, such as earnings, which can hurt option buyers even when they guess direction right. See Implied Volatility.
  • IV rank: Where current implied volatility sits within its range over a past period, usually a year. See Implied Volatility.

J–K

  • Jito tip: An extra payment on Solana to a block-building network to have a transaction included quickly or in a protected bundle. See MEV & Sandwich Attacks.
  • Journal (trading journal): A record of each trade, the reasons for it and its outcome, used to review and improve. See Trading Journal.
  • Junk bond: A high-yield bond with a credit rating below investment grade. See Bonds & Rates.
  • Kalshi: A US prediction market exchange regulated by the CFTC as a designated contract market. See Platforms Compared.
  • Kelly criterion: A formula for the bet size that maximizes long-run growth given an edge and odds. For a binary bet, the fraction is p − q ÷ b. See Kelly Criterion.
  • Keltner Channels: Bands set a multiple of ATR above and below a moving average. See ATR & Volatility.
  • Kill switch: A control that immediately stops a trading bot and cancels its orders. See Building Trading Bots.
  • Kill zone: In smart money concepts, a time window, such as the London or New York open, believed to see key moves. See Smart Money Concepts (ICT).
  • KOL (key opinion leader): An influencer whose posts can move prices, sometimes through undisclosed paid promotion. See KOLs, Insiders & Cabals.
  • KYC (know your customer): Identity checks that regulated financial firms must carry out on clients. See CEX vs DEX.

L

  • Lagging indicator: An indicator based on past prices that confirms moves after they start, such as a moving average.
  • Latency: The delay between an event, such as a price change, and a system's response to it. See High-Frequency Trading.
  • Launchpad: A platform where anyone can create and trade a new token, often on a bonding curve. See Launchpads & Bonding Curves.
  • Level 1 and Level 2 data: Level 1 shows the best bid, best ask and last trade; Level 2 shows resting orders at multiple prices. See Order Book & Depth.
  • Leverage: Using borrowed money or a derivative to control a position larger than your own capital, which magnifies gains and losses. See Leverage & Margin.
  • Leveraged ETF: An ETF that aims to deliver a multiple, such as two or three times, of its index's daily return. Over time it can drift from that multiple. See ETFs & Index Trading.
  • Limit order: An order to buy or sell at a set price or better. See Order Types.
  • Limit order book: The list of all resting buy and sell limit orders for an asset, sorted by price. See Order Book & Depth.
  • Liquidation: The forced closure of a leveraged position when its margin falls below the required level. See Liquidations.
  • Liquidation cascade: A chain reaction in which liquidations push prices further, triggering more liquidations. See Liquidations.
  • Liquidity: How easily an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price much. See Spread & Liquidity.
  • Liquidity pool: A pool of tokens locked in a smart contract that an AMM uses to quote trades. See DeFi Trading.
  • Liquidity rewards: Incentives some platforms pay to traders who keep resting orders near the market price. See Market Making (Prediction Markets).
  • Liquidity sweep: A quick move through an obvious high or low that triggers stops before reversing. See Smart Money Concepts (ICT).
  • Long position: Owning or buying an asset to profit if its price rises. See Long & Short.
  • Long squeeze: A sharp drop that forces leveraged long holders to sell, pushing prices lower still.
  • Long-shot bias: The tendency for unlikely outcomes to be overpriced in betting and prediction markets. See Pricing & Probability.
  • Look-ahead bias: A backtesting error that uses information that would not have been available at the time of the trade. See Backtesting.
  • Loss aversion: The tendency to feel losses more strongly than equal gains. See Cognitive Biases.
  • Lot: A standard unit of trade size. In forex, a standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency, a mini lot 10,000 and a micro lot 1,000. See Pips, Lots & Leverage.
  • LP burned or locked: In meme coins, liquidity pool tokens that have been destroyed or time-locked so the creator cannot withdraw the pool's liquidity. See Meme Coin Due Diligence.

M

  • MACD: An indicator built from the difference between a 12-period and 26-period EMA, with a 9-period signal line. See MACD.
  • Maintenance margin: The minimum equity required to keep a leveraged position open. See Leverage & Margin.
  • Maker and taker: A maker adds liquidity with a resting order; a taker removes it by trading against one. Many venues charge takers more. See Trading Costs & Fees.
  • Margin: Collateral deposited to open and hold a leveraged position. See Leverage & Margin.
  • Margin call: A demand to add funds when account equity falls below the maintenance requirement. See Leverage & Margin.
  • Mark price: A fair-value price, usually derived from an index, that crypto derivatives exchanges use to calculate unrealized profit and liquidations. See Perpetual Futures.
  • Mark-to-market: Revaluing positions at current market prices, daily for futures. It is also the name of a tax election for some traders. See Futures Basics.
  • Market capitalization: A company's share price multiplied by its number of shares outstanding. See Stock Market Basics.
  • Market maker: A participant who continuously quotes both buy and sell prices, earning the spread and providing liquidity. See Market Making.
  • Market order: An order to buy or sell immediately at the best available price. See Order Types.
  • Market participants: The different groups that trade, including retail traders, institutions, hedgers, market makers and central banks. See Market Participants.
  • Market profile: A charting method that shows how much time price spent at each level, using time-price opportunities (TPOs). See Volume & Market Profile.
  • Mean reversion: The tendency of a price or spread to return toward an average after moving away from it. See Mean Reversion.
  • Meme coin: A cryptocurrency whose value rests mostly on attention, community and jokes rather than any business. Most new meme coins go to zero. See Meme Coins Explained.
  • MEV (maximal extractable value): Profit that block producers or bots can capture by reordering, inserting or excluding transactions in a block. See MEV & Sandwich Attacks.
  • MiCA: The EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation, which sets rules for crypto-asset issuers and service providers. See Regulators & Investor Protection.
  • Micro E-mini: Smaller index futures contracts, one-tenth the size of the corresponding E-mini. See Index Futures (ES, NQ).
  • Midpoint: The price halfway between the best bid and best ask. See Spread & Liquidity.
  • Mint authority: A permission that lets a token's creator issue new tokens, diluting holders. See Meme Coin Due Diligence.
  • Momentum: The tendency of assets that have risen or fallen recently to keep moving in the same direction for a time. See Momentum Trading.
  • Monte Carlo simulation: Running many randomized versions of a strategy's trade sequence to estimate the range of possible outcomes. See Drawdown & Risk of Ruin.
  • Morning star and evening star: Three-candle reversal patterns at the bottom or top of a move. See Candlestick Patterns.
  • Moving average: The average price over a set number of past periods, plotted as a line. See Moving Averages.
  • MT4 and MT5: MetaTrader 4 and 5, widely used retail trading platforms, especially for forex and CFDs. See Platforms & Tools.
  • Multi-outcome market: A prediction market with more than two possible outcomes, usually structured as linked binary contracts. See Prediction Markets Explained.
  • Multi-timeframe analysis: Using a higher timeframe for direction and a lower one for timing entries. See Multi-Timeframe Analysis.
  • MVRV: The ratio of a crypto asset's market value to its realized value, used to judge whether holders are broadly in profit. See On-Chain Analysis.

N

  • Naked option: Selling an option without owning the underlying or a hedging position. Naked calls carry theoretically unlimited risk.
  • Negative balance protection: A broker policy that stops retail client losses from exceeding their account balance. See Forex Brokers.
  • News trading: Trading around scheduled data releases or breaking headlines. See News Trading.
  • NFA: The National Futures Association, the US self-regulatory organization for the derivatives industry. See Regulators & Investor Protection.
  • NFT (non-fungible token): A unique token on a blockchain, often representing digital art or collectibles. See NFT Trading.
  • No-action letter: A statement from regulator staff that they will not recommend enforcement for a specific activity under stated conditions. Some prediction markets have operated under them. See Regulation.
  • Nonfarm payrolls (NFP): A monthly US report on the number of jobs added outside farming, one of the most market-moving releases. See Economic Indicators.
  • Notional value: The total value of the position a contract controls, as opposed to the margin posted.

O

  • OCO (one cancels the other): A pair of orders where filling one automatically cancels the other. See Order Types.
  • Odds: A way of expressing the chance of an outcome or the payout on a bet, in decimal, fractional or American format. See Pricing & Probability.
  • On-balance volume (OBV): A running total that adds volume on up days and subtracts it on down days. See Volume Analysis.
  • On-chain analysis: Studying public blockchain data, such as wallet flows and holder distribution, to understand market behavior. See On-Chain Analysis.
  • One percent rule: A common guideline to risk no more than about 1 percent of the account on any single trade. See Position Sizing.
  • OPEC+: The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries and allied producers, which coordinate oil output. See Oil & Energy.
  • Open interest: The number of derivative contracts currently open. See Liquidations.
  • Opening range breakout: A strategy that trades a break above or below the high or low of the first minutes of the session. See Gap Trading.
  • OPEX: Options expiration, especially the monthly expiry, which can affect underlying prices through dealer hedging. See Gamma Exposure & Dealers.
  • Optimistic oracle: An oracle design, used by UMA, that accepts a proposed answer unless someone disputes it within a challenge window. See Resolution & Oracles.
  • Option: A contract giving the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an asset at a set price by a set date. See Options Basics.
  • Oracle: A system that brings outside information, such as prices or event outcomes, onto a blockchain for smart contracts to use. See Resolution & Oracles.
  • Order block: In smart money concepts, the last opposing candle before a strong move, treated as a zone where large orders may sit. See Smart Money Concepts (ICT).
  • Order flow: The stream of buy and sell orders hitting a market, and the study of it. See Order Flow Trading.
  • OTC (over the counter): Trading directly between parties or through dealers rather than on a central exchange. See Order Book & Depth.
  • Out of the money (OTM): A call with a strike above the underlying price, or a put with a strike below it. See Options Basics.
  • Out-of-sample testing: Testing a strategy on data not used to build it. See Backtesting.
  • Overbought and oversold: Conditions where an indicator such as RSI suggests a price has risen or fallen too far, too fast. In strong trends they can persist. See RSI.
  • Overconfidence: Overestimating your skill or the accuracy of your forecasts. See Cognitive Biases.
  • Overfitting: Building a model that fits past data so closely it fails on new data. See Overfitting.
  • Overnight financing (swap): Interest charged or paid for holding a leveraged forex or CFD position past the daily rollover time. See Trading Costs & Fees.
  • Overround: The amount by which the implied probabilities of all outcomes in a market add up to more than 100 percent. See Pricing & Probability.

P

  • P/E ratio: Share price divided by earnings per share. See Valuation Ratios.
  • Pairs trading: Buying one asset and shorting a related one, betting their price relationship will return to normal. See Pairs Trading & Stat Arb.
  • Paper trading: Practicing with simulated money in real market conditions. See How to Start Trading.
  • Pattern day trader (PDT) rule: A US FINRA rule that required at least $25,000 in a margin account for traders making four or more day trades in five business days. It was replaced by an intraday margin standard effective June 4, 2026, with brokers allowed to phase the change in until October 2027. See Day Trading Stocks.
  • Payment for order flow (PFOF): Payments a broker receives from market makers for routing client orders to them. See Trading Costs & Fees.
  • PCE (personal consumption expenditures) price index: A US inflation measure favored by the Federal Reserve. See Economic Indicators.
  • PEG ratio: The P/E ratio divided by the expected earnings growth rate. See Valuation Ratios.
  • Penny stock: A low-priced, often thinly traded share of a small company, prone to volatility and manipulation. See Penny Stocks & Small Caps.
  • Perpetual futures (perps): Crypto futures with no expiry date, kept close to spot through funding payments. See Perpetual Futures.
  • Physical settlement: Settling a contract by delivering the actual underlying asset. See Futures Basics.
  • Pig butchering: A long-con scam in which fraudsters build a relationship with a victim, then steer them into a fake trading platform. See Trading Scams.
  • Pin bar: A candle with a long wick and small body, showing a sharp rejection of a price level. See Candlestick Patterns.
  • Pin risk: Uncertainty about assignment when the underlying closes very near an option's strike at expiry. See Expiration & Assignment.
  • Pip and pipette: A pip is the standard price increment in forex, usually 0.0001, or 0.01 for yen pairs; a pipette is one tenth of a pip. See Pips, Lots & Leverage.
  • PMI (purchasing managers' index): A survey-based indicator of business activity, where readings above 50 signal expansion. See Economic Indicators.
  • Point of control (POC): The price level with the most traded volume in a volume profile. See Volume & Market Profile.
  • Polymarket: A crypto-based prediction market that runs a central limit order book with settlement on the Polygon blockchain. See Platforms Compared.
  • Ponzi scheme: A fraud that pays earlier investors with money from newer ones, often disguised as a trading or "AI bot" program. See Trading Scams.
  • Position sizing: Deciding how large each trade should be, based on account size and risk. See Position Sizing.
  • Post-earnings announcement drift: The documented tendency of stock prices to keep moving in the direction of an earnings surprise for weeks afterward. See Earnings Trading.
  • Post-only order: A limit order that is canceled rather than executed if it would take liquidity, ensuring maker status. See Order Types.
  • POV (percentage of volume): An execution algorithm that trades as a fixed share of market volume. See Execution Algorithms.
  • Predict.fun: A crypto-based prediction market on BNB Chain that uses USDT collateral and runs short crypto up/down rounds settled on a Chainlink price stream. See Platforms Compared.
  • Prediction market: An exchange for trading contracts whose payoff depends on real-world outcomes, with prices that act as probability estimates. See Prediction Markets Explained.
  • PredictIt: A US political prediction market that has operated under a CFTC no-action letter since 2014. See Platforms Compared.
  • Premarket: Trading in a stock before the regular session opens. See Pre-Market & After-Hours.
  • Premium (option): The price paid for an option. See Options Basics.
  • Price action: Trading decisions based on raw price movement and chart structure rather than indicators. See Price Action Trading.
  • Price-time priority: An order book rule that fills the best-priced orders first and, at the same price, the earliest. See Order Book & Depth.
  • Priority fee: An extra fee paid to get a blockchain transaction processed faster. See MEV & Sandwich Attacks.
  • Profit factor: Gross profits divided by gross losses. A value above 1 means a strategy made money before other considerations. See Backtesting.
  • Profit split: The share of profits a prop firm pays a funded trader. See Prop Firms & Funded Accounts.
  • Proof of reserves: An exchange's attempt to show it holds enough assets to cover customer balances. See CEX vs DEX.
  • Prop firm: A proprietary trading firm that trades its own capital or, in the online model, sells evaluations for funded accounts. See Prop Firms & Funded Accounts.
  • Pullback: A temporary move against the prevailing trend.
  • Pump and dump: A manipulation scheme that hypes an asset to raise its price, then sells to the buyers it attracted. See Trading Scams.
  • Put option: A contract giving the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to sell the underlying at the strike price. See Options Basics.
  • Put/call ratio: The volume or open interest of puts divided by that of calls, used as a sentiment gauge. See Sentiment Indicators.

Q

  • Quantitative easing (QE) and tightening (QT): Central bank programs that expand or shrink the balance sheet by buying or running off bonds. See Central Banks & Rates.
  • Quant trading: Trading based on mathematical and statistical models. See Algorithmic Trading Intro.
  • Queue position: An order's place in line at its price level, which decides when it fills. See Market Making.
  • Quote currency: The second currency in a forex pair, in which the price is expressed. In EUR/USD, the dollar is the quote currency. See Currency Pairs.

R

  • R multiple: A trade's profit or loss expressed as a multiple of the amount initially risked. See Risk/Reward & Expectancy.
  • Range: A period when price moves sideways between support and resistance. See Trends & Market Structure.
  • Realized price: In on-chain analysis, the average price at which all coins last moved, an estimate of holders' cost basis. See On-Chain Analysis.
  • Rebate: A payment some venues make to makers for adding liquidity. See Trading Costs & Fees.
  • Recency bias: Giving too much weight to recent events. See Cognitive Biases.
  • Recovery scam: A scam targeting fraud victims with false offers to recover their lost money for a fee. See Trading Scams.
  • Reduce-only order: An order that can only shrink an existing position, never open or add to one. See Order Types.
  • Reg SHO and the short-sale restriction (SSR): US rules on short selling, including a temporary limit on shorting a stock on a downtick after a large one-day fall. See Short Selling.
  • Regime: A market's prevailing behavior, such as trending, ranging, calm or volatile. See Trends & Market Structure.
  • Relative volume (RVOL): Current volume compared with the average for the same time of day. See Momentum Trading.
  • Renko chart: A chart that plots bricks of fixed price size, ignoring time. See Technical Analysis Intro.
  • Resistance: A price level where selling has tended to stop rises. See Support & Resistance.
  • Resolution: The process that decides a prediction market's winning outcome. See Resolution & Oracles.
  • Resolution source: The data source or publication a prediction market's rules name for deciding the outcome. See Resolution & Oracles.
  • Resolution sniping: Buying near-certain prediction market outcomes shortly before settlement to collect the last few cents. See Strategies Overview.
  • Retracement: A partial reversal of a prior move before the trend resumes. See Fibonacci Tools.
  • Revenge trading: Trading impulsively to win back recent losses. See FOMO & Revenge Trading.
  • Rho: How much an option's price changes for a change in interest rates. See The Greeks.
  • Risk capital: Money you can afford to lose without affecting your living costs or goals. See How to Start Trading.
  • Risk of ruin: The probability of losing so much that you can no longer trade. See Drawdown & Risk of Ruin.
  • Risk per trade: The amount you stand to lose if a trade hits its stop. See Risk Management Basics.
  • Risk-on and risk-off: Market moods in which investors favor riskier assets or flee to safer ones. See Intermarket Analysis.
  • Risk/reward ratio: The potential loss on a trade compared with its potential gain. See Risk/Reward & Expectancy.
  • Rollover (forex): The daily process of carrying open forex positions to the next value date, which triggers swap charges or credits. See Carry Trade.
  • Rollover (futures): Closing an expiring futures contract and opening a later one to keep exposure. See Contango, Backwardation & Rollover.
  • Round trip: Opening and then closing a position, incurring costs on both sides.
  • RSI (Relative Strength Index): A momentum oscillator from 0 to 100, commonly over 14 periods, with 70 and 30 often used as overbought and oversold levels. See RSI.
  • Rug pull: A scam in which a project's creators drain its liquidity or dump their tokens, leaving holders with worthless coins. See Rug Pulls & Honeypots.

S

  • Sandwich attack: An MEV tactic in which a bot buys just before a victim's swap and sells just after, profiting from the price impact. See MEV & Sandwich Attacks.
  • Scalping: A style of trading that aims for very small gains on many short trades. See Trading Styles.
  • Screener: A tool that filters assets by criteria such as price, volume or valuation. See Screeners & Scanners.
  • SEC: The US Securities and Exchange Commission, which regulates securities markets. See Regulators & Investor Protection.
  • Section 1256 contracts: A US tax category, including regulated futures and broad-based index options, whose gains are generally taxed 60 percent long-term and 40 percent short-term. See Trading Taxes Overview.
  • Seed phrase: A list of words that can restore a crypto wallet. Anyone who has it controls the funds. See Wallet Security.
  • Sector rotation: Money moving from one industry group or crypto narrative to another. See Crypto Market Cycles.
  • Session: A period of active trading in a market, such as the London or New York forex session. See Forex Sessions.
  • Settlement cycle: The time between a trade and the final exchange of cash and securities. US stocks moved to T+1, one business day, in 2024. See Day Trading Stocks.
  • Sharpe ratio: Return above the risk-free rate divided by the volatility of returns. See Backtesting.
  • Short interest: The number of shares sold short and not yet covered. See Short Selling.
  • Short position: A position that profits if the price falls, created by selling borrowed assets or using derivatives. See Long & Short.
  • Short squeeze: A rapid price rise that forces short sellers to buy back, pushing the price higher still. See Short Selling.
  • Slippage: The difference between the expected price of a trade and the price actually received. See Spread & Liquidity.
  • Slippage tolerance: The maximum price movement a DEX swap will accept before failing. Setting it too high invites sandwich attacks. See MEV & Sandwich Attacks.
  • SMA (simple moving average): The plain average of closing prices over a set number of periods. See Moving Averages.
  • Smart money concepts (SMC): A popular set of terms, such as order blocks and liquidity sweeps, that claims to track institutional activity. Independent evidence for it is lacking. See Smart Money Concepts (ICT).
  • Smart order routing: Automatically sending orders to the venue offering the best price or liquidity. See Execution Algorithms.
  • Sniping: Buying a new token in the first moments after launch. Most snipers lose to faster insiders. See Sniping Launches.
  • SOFR: The Secured Overnight Financing Rate, a benchmark US interest rate that replaced LIBOR for dollar contracts. See Bonds & Rates.
  • SOPR (spent output profit ratio): An on-chain metric showing whether coins moved on a given day were sold at a profit or a loss on average. See On-Chain Analysis.
  • Sortino ratio: Like the Sharpe ratio, but penalizing only downside volatility. See Backtesting.
  • Spoofing: Placing orders you intend to cancel to create a false impression of supply or demand. It is illegal in regulated markets. See Order Book & Depth.
  • Spot: The market for immediate purchase and delivery of an asset, as opposed to derivatives. See Crypto Trading Basics.
  • Spread betting: A UK and Irish derivative in which you bet an amount per point of price movement. See CFDs & Spread Betting.
  • Spring and upthrust: In Wyckoff analysis, false breaks below support (spring) or above resistance (upthrust) that quickly reverse. See Elliott Wave & Wyckoff.
  • Stablecoin: A cryptocurrency designed to hold a steady value, usually one US dollar. See Stablecoins.
  • Statistical arbitrage: Systematic trading of many small, statistically based price relationships. See Pairs Trading & Stat Arb.
  • Stop hunting: Price moves, real or perceived, that trigger clusters of stop-loss orders before reversing. See Stop-Loss Strategies.
  • Stop-limit order: A stop order that becomes a limit order once triggered, so it may not fill in a fast market. See Order Types.
  • Stop-loss order: An order to exit a position once price reaches a set level, limiting the loss. See Stop-Loss Strategies.
  • Stop-out: The forced closure of positions by a broker when margin falls to a set level. See Leverage & Margin.
  • STP (straight-through processing): A broker model that passes client orders to liquidity providers without a dealing desk. See Forex Brokers.
  • Straddle: Buying or selling a call and a put at the same strike and expiry, a bet on how much rather than which way a price moves. See Straddles & Strangles.
  • Strangle: Like a straddle but using out-of-the-money options at two different strikes. See Straddles & Strangles.
  • Strike price: The price at which an option can be exercised. See Options Basics.
  • Sunk cost fallacy: Continuing with a losing position because of what has already been spent on it. See Cognitive Biases.
  • Support: A price level where buying has tended to stop declines. See Support & Resistance.
  • Survivorship bias: Drawing conclusions only from the winners that remain, such as surviving stocks or profitable traders, while ignoring those that failed. See Backtesting.
  • Swing trading: Holding positions for several days to weeks to capture a price swing. See Swing Trading Stocks.
  • Sybil attack: Using many fake identities or wallets to claim more than a fair share of an airdrop or reward. See Airdrops & Points.

T

  • Take-profit order: An order that closes a position once it reaches a set profit level. See Stop-Loss Strategies.
  • Tape reading (time and sales): Watching the live record of executed trades to judge buying and selling pressure. See Order Flow Trading.
  • Technical analysis: Studying price and volume charts to make trading decisions. See Technical Analysis Intro.
  • Theta: How much an option's value falls each day from the passage of time, all else equal. See The Greeks.
  • Three white soldiers and three black crows: Patterns of three strong consecutive candles in the same direction. See Candlestick Patterns.
  • Tick size and tick value: The minimum price increment of a contract and the money that one increment is worth. An E-mini S&P 500 tick is 0.25 index points, worth $12.50. See Futures Basics.
  • Tilt: An emotional state, often after losses, in which a trader makes reckless decisions. See Handling Losses & Tilt.
  • Time in force: An order instruction setting how long it stays active, such as DAY, GTC, IOC or FOK (fill or kill). See Order Types.
  • Token approval: Permission granted to a smart contract to spend tokens from your wallet. Unlimited approvals left open are a common attack route. See Wallet Security.
  • Token unlock: The scheduled release of previously locked tokens, often to team members and investors, which can add selling pressure. See Tokenomics & Unlocks.
  • Tokenomics: The supply, distribution and incentive design of a crypto token. See Tokenomics & Unlocks.
  • Trading plan: A written set of rules covering what, when and how much you trade, and how you review performance. See Writing a Trading Plan.
  • Trailing drawdown: A prop firm loss limit that rises with the account's peak balance. See Prop Firms & Funded Accounts.
  • Trailing stop: A stop-loss that moves with price in the trade's favor by a set distance. See Stop-Loss Strategies.
  • Treasury: A debt security issued by the US government, such as T-bills, notes and bonds. See Bonds & Rates.
  • Trend following: Systematically buying assets in uptrends and selling those in downtrends. See Trend Following.
  • Trendline: A line connecting rising lows or falling highs, used to define a trend. See Trends & Market Structure.
  • Triangle: A chart pattern of converging trendlines, classed as ascending, descending or symmetrical. See Chart Patterns.
  • Triangular arbitrage: Exploiting price inconsistencies among three currency or crypto pairs. See Crypto Arbitrage.
  • TWAP (time-weighted average price): An execution algorithm that splits an order evenly over a period of time. See Execution Algorithms.

U

  • UMA: A crypto protocol whose optimistic oracle resolves most Polymarket markets, with disputes escalating to token-holder votes. See Resolution & Oracles.
  • Underlying: The asset, rate or index on which a derivative is based.
  • Unrealized profit or loss: The gain or loss on an open position at current prices, not yet locked in by closing.
  • Up/down round: A short prediction market, often 5 or 15 minutes, on whether a crypto asset's price will finish higher or lower than it started. See Strategies Overview.
  • USDC and USDT: The two largest US dollar stablecoins, widely used as trading collateral. See Stablecoins.

V

  • Value area: The price range containing about 70 percent of a session's volume in a volume profile. See Volume & Market Profile.
  • Vanna and charm: Second-order option Greeks describing how delta changes with volatility (vanna) and with time (charm). See Gamma Exposure & Dealers.
  • Vega: How much an option's price changes for a one-point change in implied volatility. See The Greeks.
  • Vertical spread: Buying and selling options of the same type and expiry at different strikes. See Vertical Spreads.
  • Vesting cliff: A date before which no locked tokens are released, after which a large batch may unlock at once. See Tokenomics & Unlocks.
  • VIX: An index of the 30-day implied volatility of S&P 500 options, often called the "fear gauge." See Volatility & VIX.
  • Volatility: How much and how quickly an asset's price moves. See ATR & Volatility.
  • Volatility decay: The erosion of leveraged and inverse ETF returns relative to their target multiple when prices swing back and forth. See ETFs & Index Trading.
  • Volatility skew: The pattern of different implied volatilities across strikes of the same expiry. See Implied Volatility.
  • Volmageddon: The February 2018 spike in volatility that wiped out several short-volatility products. See Volatility & VIX.
  • Volume: The number of shares, contracts or coins traded over a period. See Volume Analysis.
  • Volume profile: A histogram showing how much volume traded at each price level. See Volume & Market Profile.
  • VWAP (volume-weighted average price): The average price of an asset weighted by volume over a session, used as an intraday benchmark. See VWAP.

W

  • Walk-forward analysis: Repeatedly optimizing a strategy on one period and testing it on the next to check robustness. See Backtesting.
  • WASDE: The US Department of Agriculture's monthly World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates report. See Commodity Futures.
  • Wash sale rule: A US tax rule that disallows a loss if you buy a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after selling it. See Trading Taxes Overview.
  • Wash trading: Trading with yourself to create fake volume, common on unregulated crypto venues and NFT markets. See Altcoin Trading.
  • Wedge: A chart pattern of two converging trendlines both sloping up or down. See Chart Patterns.
  • Whale: A trader or wallet large enough to move prices. See Market Participants.
  • Wheel strategy: Repeatedly selling cash-secured puts and, if assigned, covered calls on the same stock. See Covered Calls & CSPs.
  • Whipsaw: A sharp move one way quickly followed by a reversal, often stopping out traders on both sides.
  • Whisper number: An unofficial earnings expectation circulating among traders that may differ from the published consensus. See Earnings & Financials.
  • Wick (shadow): The thin line on a candlestick showing the high or low beyond the body. See Reading Candlesticks.
  • Win rate: The percentage of trades that make money. A high win rate does not guarantee profit. See Risk/Reward & Expectancy.
  • Wisdom of crowds: The idea that combining many independent estimates can produce a more accurate forecast than most individuals. See Prediction Markets Explained.
  • WTI (West Texas Intermediate): A US crude oil benchmark, the basis for the main NYMEX crude futures contract. See Oil & Energy.
  • Wyckoff method: A framework describing accumulation and distribution phases driven by large operators. See Elliott Wave & Wyckoff.

X–Z

  • XAU/USD: The price of one troy ounce of gold in US dollars. See Gold & Precious Metals.
  • YES and NO shares: The two sides of a binary prediction market. One pays $1 at resolution and the other pays nothing. See Pricing & Probability.
  • Yield: The income return on an investment, such as a bond's interest relative to its price. See Bonds & Rates.
  • Yield curve: A plot of interest rates across bond maturities. An inverted curve, with short rates above long rates, has often preceded US recessions. See Bonds & Rates.
  • Z-score: How many standard deviations a value is from its mean, used to time mean-reversion entries. See Mean Reversion.
  • Zero-days-to-expiry (0DTE) option: An option traded on its expiration day. These options are highly sensitive to small price moves. See 0DTE Options.
  • Zero-sum game: A situation where one participant's gain is another's loss. Trading after costs is negative-sum for participants as a group. See What Is Trading?.
