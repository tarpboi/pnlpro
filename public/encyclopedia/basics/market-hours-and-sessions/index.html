Market Hours and Sessions

7 min read · Updated Nov 23, 2026

Market hours are the times when an exchange or market accepts orders and trades, and sessions are the recurring blocks of activity within them, such as the US stock market's regular session or the London forex session. Hours range from a few hours a day on many stock exchanges to nonstop trading in crypto. Liquidity, spreads and volatility change sharply across the day and week, so when you trade can matter as much as what you trade.

Key takeaways

  • Most stock exchanges have a regular daytime session plus limited pre-market and after-hours trading with thinner liquidity.
  • Forex trades 24 hours a day from Sunday evening to Friday evening (New York time), moving through the Sydney, Tokyo, London and New York sessions.
  • Many futures trade nearly 24 hours on weekdays, with a short daily maintenance break.
  • Crypto trades 24/7, but liquidity still follows a daily and weekly rhythm.
  • Markets that close for the weekend can reopen at a very different price, and daylight saving changes shift hours by an hour on different dates in different countries.

Stock exchange hours

Each stock exchange sets its own hours in local time. The table below shows regular sessions for some major exchanges, as of 2026. Exchanges occasionally change their hours, so check the current schedule.

Exchange Local regular hours Approximate UTC (northern winter) Notes
New York Stock Exchange, Nasdaq 9:30 to 16:00 ET 14:30 to 21:00 13:30 to 20:00 UTC when US daylight time is in effect
London Stock Exchange 08:00 to 16:30 UK time 08:00 to 16:30 One hour earlier in UTC during UK summer time
Deutsche Börse Xetra (Frankfurt) 09:00 to 17:30 CET 08:00 to 16:30 One hour earlier in UTC during EU summer time
Tokyo Stock Exchange 9:00 to 11:30 and 12:30 to 15:30 JST 00:00 to 02:30 and 03:30 to 06:30 Japan does not use daylight saving time
Hong Kong Exchanges 9:30 to 12:00 and 13:00 to 16:00 HKT 01:30 to 04:00 and 05:00 to 08:00 No daylight saving time

Most exchanges also run opening and closing auctions, short periods where orders are collected and matched at a single price. The closing auction is especially important because many funds trade at the official closing price.

Pre-market and after-hours

US stocks can also trade outside the regular session through extended-hours sessions. Many US brokers offer pre-market trading from around 4:00 ET and after-hours trading until around 20:00 ET, and some now offer overnight trading in selected stocks. Several US venues have announced or launched plans for longer trading days; check the current status.

Extended-hours trading tends to have far fewer participants, wider spreads and sharper moves, especially when earnings or news are released outside regular hours. Many brokers accept only limit orders in these sessions. See pre-market and after-hours trading.

Forex: 24 hours, five days a week

The foreign exchange market has no single exchange, so it has no single opening bell. Trading follows the business day around the world, starting with the opening of Asia-Pacific markets on Sunday evening New York time (Monday morning in Sydney) and ending at 17:00 New York time on Friday.

Traders usually describe four sessions. Times are approximate, because each financial center's activity tapers in and out and daylight saving shifts them.

Session Approximate UTC Characteristics
Sydney 21:00 to 06:00 Quietest; Australian and New Zealand dollars most active
Tokyo 00:00 to 09:00 Yen pairs active; often range-bound in majors
London 07:00 to 16:00 Highest volume; many trends start here
New York 12:00 to 21:00 US data releases; active until London closes

The London-New York overlap, roughly 12:00 to 16:00 UTC, is usually the most liquid period of the day, with the tightest spreads in major pairs. The hour around the daily rollover at 17:00 New York time is usually the least liquid, and spreads often widen sharply. Session details are covered in forex trading sessions.

Futures: nearly 24 hours with a break

Many major futures contracts trade electronically almost around the clock on weekdays. On CME Group's Globex platform, for example, many equity index, currency, energy and metals contracts trade from Sunday evening to Friday afternoon US Central time, with a daily break of about one hour. Hours differ by contract and exchange, and agricultural futures have shorter sessions.

Futures volume is still concentrated in the hours when the underlying cash market is open. Stock index futures trade most heavily during the regular stock session, and overnight moves happen on much thinner volume. See index futures.

Crypto: 24/7

Crypto markets never close. Centralized exchanges and on-chain protocols run through nights, weekends and holidays. That convenience has costs:

  • Liquidity still varies. Volume and depth tend to be lower on weekends and during Asian late-night hours for Western-focused markets, so spreads can widen and moves can be sharper.
  • No pause to reflect. Positions can be liquidated while you sleep.
  • Weekend gaps elsewhere. Exchange-traded crypto products, such as CME bitcoin futures and spot ETFs, close on weekends while crypto keeps trading, so they can open at very different prices on Monday.

Weekend gap risk

When a market closes for the weekend, news keeps happening. The next session can open far from Friday's close, a jump called a gap. Stop orders do not protect you through a gap; they trigger at the first available price.

Worked example: a weekend gap in forex

A trader is long one standard lot of EUR/USD (pip value $10) from 1.0800, with a stop at 1.0780, risking 20 pips or $200.

The market closes on Friday at 1.0800. Over the weekend, unexpected political news breaks, and the market reopens on Sunday evening at 1.0750.

  • The stop at 1.0780 is skipped, and it fills at the first available price, about 1.0750.
  • Actual loss: 1.0800 - 1.0750 = 50 pips, or 50 x $10 = $500.
  • That is 2.5 times the planned $200 risk.

Gaps also occur in stocks overnight and around earnings, in futures after the daily break and after weekends, and in any market after a trading halt; see gap trading. Traders who hold over weekends often reduce size to account for gap risk.

Holidays

Exchanges close on national public holidays, and schedules differ by country. US stock exchanges close on holidays such as Independence Day and Thanksgiving, and some close early on the day before or after certain holidays. UK, European and Asian exchanges have their own calendars, including multi-day closures around Lunar New Year in some Asian markets and around Christmas and New Year in Europe.

Forex technically trades on most holidays, but liquidity thins when a major center is closed. The period between Christmas and New Year is typically very thin, and thin markets can produce sudden spikes. Always check exchange holiday calendars for the coming year.

Daylight saving time shifts

Daylight saving time (DST) causes confusion twice a year because countries switch on different dates.

  • The US moves clocks forward on the second Sunday of March and back on the first Sunday of November.
  • The UK and EU move clocks forward on the last Sunday of March and back on the last Sunday of October.
  • Japan, China and Hong Kong do not use daylight saving time.
  • Australia's DST runs roughly from October to April, opposite to the northern hemisphere.

For two to three weeks each spring and about one week each autumn, the gap between New York and London is four hours instead of the usual five. During those weeks, the US open, US data releases and the London-New York overlap all fall an hour earlier or later than usual for traders in Europe and Asia. Traders who schedule by UTC should recheck times whenever any of these switches happen.

Common mistakes

  • Trading illiquid hours with tight stops. Wide spreads at night or at rollover can trigger stops on noise.
  • Holding leveraged positions over weekends without adjusting size. Gaps can exceed planned risk several times over.
  • Forgetting holidays. Thin markets on half-days and holidays move unpredictably.
  • Ignoring DST mismatches. A routine built around the US open can be off by an hour for part of the year.
  • Assuming 24/7 means always liquid. Crypto weekends can be thin and volatile.

FAQ

What are the US stock market hours?

The regular session for the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq runs from 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time on weekdays. Many brokers also offer pre-market and after-hours trading with thinner liquidity.

What is the best time to trade forex?

Liquidity is usually highest during the London session and the London-New York overlap, roughly 12:00 to 16:00 UTC. Spreads are tightest then, though the best time for a given strategy depends on the pairs traded.

Do futures trade 24 hours?

Many major futures trade nearly 24 hours a day on weekdays, with a short daily break and a weekend closure. Hours vary by contract, and some agricultural futures have much shorter sessions.

Does crypto trade on weekends?

Yes. Crypto trades 24 hours a day, seven days a week, including holidays. Liquidity is often lower on weekends, which can lead to wider spreads and sharper moves.
