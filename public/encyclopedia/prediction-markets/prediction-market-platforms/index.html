Prediction Market Platforms Compared

7 min read · Updated Nov 23, 2026
More in Prediction Markets

Prediction market platforms fall into two broad groups: regulated exchanges that operate in national currency under a financial regulator, and crypto-based venues that settle trades on a blockchain using stablecoins. They differ in who can use them, how outcomes are decided, what they charge and which events they list. This article describes the main platforms neutrally as of November 2026. It does not rank them, and because access rules and fees change often, check each platform's current terms before signing up.

Key takeaways

  • Kalshi is a CFTC-regulated exchange that trades in US dollars, while Polymarket, Predict.fun and Limitless settle on blockchains using stablecoin collateral.
  • How a platform resolves markets, whether by its own rules team or by an on-chain oracle, is as important as its fees.
  • Liquidity is concentrated in a few venues and a few popular markets, so smaller markets can have wide spreads.
  • Access is restricted by country, and in the US sometimes by state; getting around a geoblock usually breaches a platform's terms.
  • Play-money sites such as Manifold are a low-cost way to practice forecasting without risking money.

The main platforms

Polymarket

Polymarket, founded in 2020, is the largest crypto-based prediction market. It runs a central limit order book with trades settled on the Polygon blockchain, using USDC-style stablecoin collateral. Most of its markets resolve through UMA's optimistic oracle, in which an outcome is proposed and can be challenged; see resolution and oracles. It lists politics, world events, economics, sports, culture and recurring crypto price markets.

Access is restricted in several countries. For US users, the company acquired a CFTC-licensed exchange in 2025 and runs a separate, more limited US product, while its main international exchange was reported in 2026 to be seeking US approval. For much of its history it charged no trading fee on most markets, while adding fees on some market types. Check its current terms, fee schedule and the list of restricted regions.

Kalshi

Kalshi is a designated contract market regulated by the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), the same kind of exchange that lists futures. Accounts are funded in US dollars, and every user goes through identity checks. It lists contracts on economic data, interest rate decisions, weather, politics, culture, crypto prices and sports. Its taker fees have followed a formula proportional to price × (1 − price), so they are highest near 50 cents.

Its sports contracts have been challenged by several US states, and federal courts had reached conflicting decisions as of November 2026. See prediction market regulation and check whether its markets are available in your state or country.

Predict.fun

Predict.fun is a crypto-based prediction market on BNB Chain that uses USDT as collateral. Alongside event markets, it runs short 5-minute and 15-minute crypto up/down rounds, in which traders bet on whether a coin's price will be higher or lower at the end of the window. Those rounds settle on a Chainlink price stream. Short rounds settle fast and repeat constantly, which suits automated traders and also carries a gambling-like rhythm worth being honest with yourself about. Check its terms for access restrictions.

PredictIt

PredictIt launched in 2014, run with a New Zealand university under a CFTC no-action letter, and focuses on US political markets. For years it limited each trader's stake per contract and the number of traders per contract. A 2025 agreement with the CFTC loosened those limits, and its operator separately obtained approval for a regulated exchange. It has historically charged a fee on profits and a fee on withdrawals. Check its current limits and fees.

Manifold

Manifold is a play-money platform. Users trade a virtual currency, and anyone can create a market on almost any question. Because no real money is at stake, it cannot be used to make a profit, but it is a useful place to practice probability estimates and see how markets react to news.

Limitless

Limitless is a crypto-based prediction market on the Base blockchain using USDC. It is known for short-dated crypto price markets as well as other event markets. As with other crypto venues, check access rules for your country.

Others

  • ForecastEx, owned by Interactive Brokers, is a CFTC-regulated venue offering event contracts on economic and other outcomes through the broker.
  • Brokerage apps, such as Robinhood, have offered event contracts to US customers, at times through a partner exchange.
  • Betting exchanges such as those licensed in the UK let users back and lay outcomes, mostly in sports, under gambling rather than financial regulation. See betting exchange trading.
  • Forecasting sites such as Metaculus collect probability forecasts and score users on accuracy without any trading.

How to compare platforms for yourself

What to look at

  1. Legality where you live. Read the terms of service and the list of restricted regions first.
  2. Liquidity in the markets you care about. Look at the order book depth, not the headline volume.
  3. All-in costs. Add trading fees, the spread, deposit and withdrawal fees, and blockchain transaction costs.
  4. Resolution process. Who decides, using which source, and what happens in a dispute.
  5. Custody and security. Who holds your money, and what happens if the platform fails. On crypto venues, wallet security is your job.
  6. Tools. Whether there is an API for automated trading, and its rate limits.

Worked example: comparing all-in cost

Imagine you want to buy 200 YES contracts on the same event, with the fair midpoint at 50 cents on two venues.

  • Venue A charges no trading fee, but its spread is 49 to 51 cents. You pay 200 × $0.51 = $102.00.
  • Venue B has a tighter spread of 49.5 to 50.5 cents but charges a taker fee of 0.07 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 1.75 cents per contract. You pay 200 × $0.505 = $101.00, plus 200 × $0.0175 = $3.50 in fees, for $104.50.

Here the "free" venue is $2.50 cheaper. On a thinner market where Venue A's spread widened to 46 to 54 cents, it would cost 200 × $0.54 = $108.00, and Venue B would be cheaper. The lesson is to compare the total cost of the specific trade, not the fee headline. The article on trading costs and fees applies the same idea across markets.

Common mistakes

  • Using a VPN to get around a geoblock. It usually breaches the terms of service and can lead to frozen funds, as well as possible legal trouble.
  • Assuming the same event means the same contract. Two platforms can word, time and source the "same" market differently.
  • Leaving large balances on a platform. Keep only what you are actively trading, whatever the venue.
  • Judging liquidity by volume. A market with big lifetime volume can still have an empty book today.
  • Ignoring stablecoin and chain risk. Collateral in USDT or USDC carries its own stablecoin risks.

FAQ

What is the difference between Polymarket and Kalshi?

Kalshi is a CFTC-regulated US exchange that trades in dollars with full identity checks. Polymarket is a crypto-based venue that settles on the Polygon blockchain with stablecoin collateral and resolves most markets through UMA's oracle. Each has different access rules, fees and market lists, so compare their current terms.

Can I use Polymarket in the US?

Polymarket's main international exchange has restricted US users for years, and the company runs a separate, more limited US product through a CFTC-licensed exchange it bought in 2025. Reports in 2026 said it was seeking approval to open the main exchange to US users. Check its current terms before trying to sign up.

Which prediction market has no fees?

Some platforms charge no trading fee on certain markets but may charge on others, or for deposits, withdrawals and blockchain transactions. A venue with no trading fee can still cost more if its spreads are wide, so compare the total cost of a trade.

Is there a prediction market without real money?

Yes. Manifold uses play money, and forecasting sites such as Metaculus score predictions without trading. They are a good way to practice estimating probabilities before risking money.
