VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price)

6 min read · Updated Nov 23, 2026

VWAP, the volume-weighted average price, is the average price an asset has traded at over a period, with each price weighted by how much volume traded there. It is most often calculated from the start of each trading session and resets daily. Institutions use it to judge the quality of their executions, and day traders use it as an intraday reference for whether buyers or sellers have been in control of the session.

Key takeaways

  • VWAP equals total traded value divided by total traded volume, accumulated from a chosen starting point.
  • Standard session VWAP resets each day, so it is mainly an intraday tool.
  • Large funds often measure their fills against VWAP, which is one reason price tends to react around it.
  • Anchored VWAP starts the calculation from a chosen event, such as an earnings gap or a major low.
  • VWAP lags, says nothing about the future on its own, and is less meaningful in markets without reliable volume data.

How VWAP is calculated

For each bar, take a representative price, usually the typical price = (High + Low + Close) ÷ 3, and multiply it by that bar's volume. Keep running totals:

VWAP = Σ (Typical price × Volume) ÷ Σ Volume

The sums start at the session open and grow with every bar. Early in the session VWAP moves a lot; by the afternoon it has so much volume behind it that it barely moves.

Worked example

Five 5-minute bars at the start of a session:

Bar Typical price Volume Price × Volume Cumulative PV Cumulative volume VWAP
1 100.00 10,000 1,000,000 1,000,000 10,000 100.00
2 100.50 8,000 804,000 1,804,000 18,000 100.22
3 101.00 12,000 1,212,000 3,016,000 30,000 100.53
4 100.80 6,000 604,800 3,620,800 36,000 100.58
5 100.20 4,000 400,800 4,021,600 40,000 100.54

After five bars, VWAP is 4,021,600 ÷ 40,000 = 100.54. The simple average of the five typical prices is 502.50 ÷ 5 = 100.50. VWAP sits slightly higher because the heaviest volume (12,000) traded at the highest price (101.00). That is the whole point: VWAP reflects where most of the business was actually done.

VWAP as an institutional benchmark

A pension fund that needs to buy 500,000 shares cannot do it in one order without moving the price. It splits the order across the day, often with a VWAP execution algorithm that tries to trade in proportion to the market's volume. Afterward, the trading desk compares its average fill price to the day's VWAP.

Example. A desk buys 50,000 shares at an average of 100.48. The session VWAP is 100.54. The desk bought 0.06 below VWAP, which on 50,000 shares is 0.06 × 50,000 = $3,000 better than the benchmark. If it had paid 100.60, it would have been $3,000 worse.

Because so much institutional flow is measured this way, buyers with large orders tend to see prices below VWAP as good value and sellers tend to see prices above VWAP as good value. That behavior is part of why VWAP can act like a magnet or a reaction level during the day, although nobody can see how much benchmarked flow is active on a given day.

How day traders use VWAP

Day traders, especially in stocks and index futures, watch VWAP in a few common ways:

  • Trend filter. Price holding above a rising VWAP is read as buyers in control for the session; below a falling VWAP, sellers. Some traders only take longs above VWAP and shorts below it.
  • Pullback entries. In a strong trending day, the first pullback to VWAP is a popular entry. The idea is the same as buying a pullback to a moving average, but VWAP is anchored to the session and weighted by volume.
  • Reclaim or loss of VWAP. A stock that opens weak, then crosses back above VWAP and holds, is sometimes treated as a sign the session's balance has shifted.
  • Mean reversion. In range-bound sessions, price often oscillates around VWAP, and stretched moves away from it can drift back.

None of these are reliable on their own. On a strong trend day price may never return to VWAP; on a choppy day it may cross VWAP a dozen times, each one a potential false signal.

Anchored VWAP

Standard VWAP starts at the session open. Anchored VWAP (AVWAP) lets you start the calculation at any bar you choose. The technique was popularized by technical analyst Brian Shannon and is now built into most charting platforms. Common anchors:

  • An earnings gap or major news day.
  • A significant swing high or low.
  • The start of a year, quarter or month.
  • An IPO date.

The logic is that AVWAP from an event shows the average cost of everyone who traded since then. If price is above an AVWAP anchored at a major low, the average buyer since that low is in profit. If price falls back to it, those buyers are at breakeven, which some traders treat as potential support. This is an intuitive story rather than a proven effect, and the choice of anchor is subjective.

VWAP bands

Many platforms plot bands around VWAP at one, two and sometimes three standard deviations of price from VWAP, weighted by volume. They look and work much like Bollinger Bands around a moving average. Traders use the outer bands as signs of a stretched move in a range day, or as evidence of strength when price keeps riding the upper band in a trend day. Some platforms offer percentage bands instead of standard-deviation bands; check which one your tool uses.

Limitations

Limitation Why it matters
Lag VWAP is an average of the past. Late in the session it hardly moves.
Daily reset Session VWAP says little about multi-day trends; that is what anchored VWAP or longer averages are for.
Data quality Different feeds and session definitions (with or without pre-market) give different VWAP values.
Weak in some markets Spot forex has no central volume, and crypto volume can be inflated by wash trading. See volume analysis.
24-hour markets Crypto and futures have no natural open, so the reset time (often 00:00 UTC for crypto) is a convention.
Not a signal Crossing VWAP is not a buy or sell signal by itself.

Common mistakes

  • Using VWAP on daily or weekly charts. Session VWAP is an intraday tool. For longer horizons use anchored VWAP or other averages.
  • Ignoring the session setting. Whether your platform includes pre-market and after-hours trading changes the number.
  • Treating every touch as a trade. VWAP works best combined with session context, levels and volume, not as a standalone trigger.
  • Assuming institutions are defending VWAP. Benchmark flow exists, but you cannot see it, and it does not guarantee a bounce.

FAQ

What does it mean when price is above VWAP?

It means the current price is higher than the volume-weighted average price paid so far in the session, so the average buyer today is in profit. Many traders read this as a sign that buyers have had the upper hand, though it predicts nothing on its own.

Does VWAP reset every day?

Standard session VWAP resets at the start of each trading session. Anchored VWAP does not reset; it runs continuously from whatever starting bar the trader chooses.

Is VWAP better than a moving average?

It is different rather than better. VWAP weights prices by volume and is tied to a session or anchor, while a moving average weights prices by time over a rolling window. Intraday traders often use VWAP; swing traders more often use moving averages.

Can you use VWAP for crypto or forex?

You can, but with care. Crypto VWAP depends on which exchanges' volume is included and on an arbitrary daily reset time, and spot forex VWAP uses tick volume from a single broker, which is only a proxy for real activity.
