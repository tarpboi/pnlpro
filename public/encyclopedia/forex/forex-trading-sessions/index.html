Forex Trading Sessions

The forex market trades around the clock for five days a week because it follows the business day across the world's financial centers, from Sydney and Tokyo to London and New York. Each session has its own level of activity, its own typical movers and its own risks. Knowing when liquidity is deep, when spreads widen and when prices can gap helps a trader choose pairs and hours that suit their strategy and their own time zone.

Key takeaways

  • The forex week runs from about 5 p.m. New York time on Sunday to 5 p.m. New York time on Friday.
  • Activity passes through four main sessions: Sydney, Tokyo, London and New York.
  • The London and New York overlap is usually the most liquid and active part of the day for the major pairs.
  • Spreads often widen around the daily rollover at 5 p.m. New York time and in the quiet hours after it.
  • Prices can gap between Friday's close and Sunday's open, and stop orders may fill well beyond their set level.

Why forex never sleeps during the week

Forex is an over-the-counter market with no single exchange, so there is no opening bell. Instead, trading moves from one region's banks and dealers to the next as the business day moves west. The week starts when Wellington and Sydney open on Monday morning local time, which is Sunday evening in New York, and ends when New York closes on Friday afternoon. The general picture of hours across all asset classes is in market hours and sessions.

The four sessions

Session times are not official. They describe roughly when each center's dealers are active. The times below are approximate and use local business hours of about 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. (7 a.m. to 4 p.m. for Sydney).

Session Local hours (approx.) UTC, northern summer UTC, northern winter
Sydney 7:00 to 16:00 21:00 to 06:00 20:00 to 05:00
Tokyo 9:00 to 18:00 00:00 to 09:00 00:00 to 09:00
London 8:00 to 17:00 07:00 to 16:00 08:00 to 17:00
New York 8:00 to 17:00 12:00 to 21:00 13:00 to 22:00

Daylight saving time shifts these times. The UK and US move their clocks on different dates in spring and autumn, Japan does not use daylight saving, and Australia changes its clocks in the opposite direction to the northern hemisphere. For a few weeks each year the gaps between sessions are an hour different from usual. Check a session clock or your platform's server time when the clocks change.

Sydney

The Sydney session opens the week. Liquidity is relatively thin, and the Australian and New Zealand dollars are the most active currencies. Monday's open is when any weekend gap first shows up.

Tokyo

The Tokyo session, often called the Asian session, brings in Japanese banks, exporters and investors, as well as activity in Singapore and Hong Kong. The yen pairs and the Australian and New Zealand dollars are usually most active. Moves in major European pairs are often smaller and range-bound during these hours, though Japanese and Chinese data releases can cause sharp moves.

London

London is the largest FX trading center in the world, and its open usually brings a jump in volume and volatility. European currencies, the pound and the Swiss franc become active, and many of the day's trends begin here. Some traders try to trade the breakout of the Asian range at the London open, described in forex trading strategies.

New York

New York brings in US banks, funds and corporate flows. Major US economic releases, such as the monthly jobs report and inflation data, usually come out at 8:30 a.m. New York time, and they can move every dollar pair at once. Activity tends to fade after London closes, and the late New York afternoon is often quiet.

The overlaps

When two major centers are open together, more dealers are quoting and more orders are flowing.

Overlap UTC, northern summer UTC, northern winter Typical activity
Tokyo and London 07:00 to 09:00 08:00 to 09:00 Short, can be lively for yen and euro crosses
London and New York 12:00 to 16:00 13:00 to 17:00 Usually the most liquid hours for majors

The London and New York overlap is when spreads on EUR/USD, GBP/USD and USD/CHF tend to be tightest and when many large moves happen. It is also when US data hits the market, so it is not a calm period.

Worked example: converting to your local time

A trader in Singapore (UTC+8, no daylight saving) wants to trade the London and New York overlap.

  • Northern summer: 12:00 to 16:00 UTC becomes 20:00 to 24:00 Singapore time.
  • Northern winter: 13:00 to 17:00 UTC becomes 21:00 to 01:00 Singapore time.

A trader in Los Angeles (UTC−8 in winter, UTC−7 in summer) sees the same overlap at about 5:00 to 9:00 a.m. local time all year, because their own clocks shift along with New York's. A useful exercise is to work out which sessions fall in your own waking hours before choosing what to trade.

Which pairs move when

These are tendencies, not rules.

Time of day Pairs often most active
Sydney and Tokyo AUD/USD, NZD/USD, USD/JPY, AUD/JPY
London morning EUR/USD, GBP/USD, EUR/GBP, USD/CHF, EUR/JPY, GBP/JPY
London and New York overlap All majors, especially EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/CAD
Late New York Quieter across the board; USD/CAD can react to oil

Pairs are generally cheapest to trade when both of their currencies' home markets are open. Trading EUR/GBP in the middle of the Asian session usually means wider spreads than trading it in the London morning. See bid-ask spread and liquidity.

Daily fixes

A fix is a benchmark exchange rate calculated at a set time, used by funds, companies and custodians to value assets and execute orders. The best known is the WM/Reuters 4 p.m. London fix, calculated from trades and quotes in a short window around 4 p.m. London time. Other widely used benchmarks include the Tokyo fix at 9:55 a.m. Tokyo time and the European Central Bank's daily reference rates, set in the early afternoon Central European time.

Large orders tied to fixes can cause short bursts of volatility, especially at month end, when investors rebalance currency hedges. In 2014 and 2015, regulators in the UK, the US and Switzerland fined several large banks billions of dollars in total over traders' attempts to manipulate FX benchmarks. Fix calculation methods were tightened afterwards.

Rollover and spread widening

The forex trading day ends at 5 p.m. New York time. At that moment, open positions are rolled to the next value date, and brokers apply the overnight financing charge or credit, called swap. This is explained in the carry trade article.

In the minutes around rollover, many liquidity providers pause or widen their prices while they roll their own books. Retail spreads can widen to several times their normal size, especially on crosses and exotics. Traders with tight stops can be stopped out by the wider spread even if the mid price barely moves. Many short-term traders avoid holding new positions in the half hour around rollover.

Because spot FX settles two business days after the trade for most pairs, the rollover on Wednesday usually covers the weekend as well. Most brokers apply three days of swap on Wednesday night. Holidays can shift this.

Weekend gaps

The market closes for about two days each weekend, but news does not stop. Elections, policy announcements and geopolitical events can move the opening price on Sunday evening well away from Friday's close. This is called a gap.

Worked example: a stop that fills past its level

A trader holds 1 standard lot of EUR/USD long from 1.0850 over a weekend, with a stop-loss at 1.0820, 30 pips below. At $10 per pip, the planned maximum loss is $300.

Weekend news pushes the Sunday open to 1.0790. A normal stop order becomes a market order once its price is touched, so it fills at or near the first available price, about 1.0790. The loss is 60 pips, or $600, double the plan. Only a guaranteed stop, offered by some brokers for a fee, would have capped the loss at $300. See stop-loss strategies.

Holidays create similar thin conditions. Around Christmas and New Year, and on major national holidays in Japan, the UK or the US, liquidity can drop and spreads can widen.

Common mistakes

  • Trading the quietest hours with a breakout strategy. Low-volume hours tend to produce false moves and wide spreads.
  • Ignoring daylight saving changes. A strategy timed to "the London open" can fire an hour early or late for a few weeks.
  • Holding tight stops through rollover. Temporary spread widening can trigger them.
  • Forgetting the Wednesday triple swap. Positions with a negative swap pay three days at once.
  • Assuming a stop caps weekend risk. Gaps can skip right past it.

FAQ

What is the best time to trade forex?

For most major pairs, the London and New York overlap is usually the most liquid period, with the tightest spreads. The best time for an individual depends on their strategy, the pairs they trade and when they can actually focus.

Is the forex market open on weekends?

The main interbank market is closed from Friday evening to Sunday evening, New York time. Some brokers offer weekend quotes on certain products, but these are usually wide and thin.

What time does the forex market open on Sunday?

It opens at about 5 p.m. New York time on Sunday, which is Monday morning in Wellington and Sydney. In UTC, that is around 21:00 in the northern summer and 22:00 in the northern winter.

Why do spreads widen at 5 p.m. New York time?

That is the daily rollover, when liquidity providers roll positions to the next value date and many pause quoting. With fewer prices in the market, spreads widen until normal quoting resumes.
